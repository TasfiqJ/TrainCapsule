- **Status:** **BUILD AUTHORIZED — proof-seeking, option-preserving company build**
- **Working codename:** TrainCapsule. Complete legal, domain, package, and trademark clearance before public release.
- **Initial category:** **Accelerated Workload Failure Reproduction and Change Qualification**

- **Source-of-truth rule:** this file and the accompanying final document set replace all earlier TrainCapsule plans where wording, scope, buyer, roadmap, or evidence standards conflict.

> **Final decision**
>
> Build TrainCapsule now. The autonomous engineering loop may construct the complete local, customer-deployable product before market proof because the founder's direct engineering-time cost is low. However, the loop is not authorized to fabricate demand, treat synthetic success as commercial proof, or expand into expensive hosted/provider breadth merely because code can be generated.
>
> The correct doctrine is:
>
> **Build the reusable truth, experiment, and qualification kernel; continuously search for the highest-value failure workflow that the kernel can close; then use real behavior to determine which commercial surface deserves focus.**

## 00.1 The company being built

TrainCapsule is not a GPU black box, observability dashboard, restart system, or AI log analyst.

> **TrainCapsule converts an unresolved accelerated-workload failure into the lowest-cost faithful experiment it can establish within a declared budget, evaluates a bounded recovery, and preserves the confirmed failure as an expiring workload incident contract that can qualify future software, hardware, topology, checkpoint, and cloud changes.**

The product has three connected loops.

### Close

```text
native evidence
→ exact workload/environment identity
→ evidence completeness and perturbation assessment
→ first observed inconsistent boundary
→ competing mechanism hypotheses
→ controlled discriminating experiments
→ minimum faithful experiment within budget
→ recovery assurance
→ failure-derived incident contract
```

The completed customer outcome is not “we found an anomaly.” It is one or more of:

- the incident is materially cheaper to execute and investigate;
- a mechanism is reproduced within a declared applicability envelope;
- a recovery decision is safer because named state properties were tested;
- a private incident becomes a durable executable or federated contract;
- or the product correctly recommends mitigation without further reproduction because deeper closure is uneconomic.

### Qualify

Confirmed incidents and explicitly purchased critical-workload controls become versioned contracts. TrainCapsule executes those contracts when the customer changes:

- PyTorch, CUDA, NCCL, compiler, driver, firmware, or container;
- accelerator generation, node type, cloud, topology, or scheduler;
- checkpoint, optimizer, RNG, sampler, or resume policy;
- workload code, parallelism graph, or data pipeline;
- or a customer-defined release boundary.

This is the recurring product. It is **not** a generic compatibility matrix. Every recurring contract must come from:

1. a confirmed failure;
2. a customer-approved recovery-state property;
3. or a specifically purchased critical-workload qualification requirement with objective pass/fail semantics.

Ordinary repository bugs that collapse into one stable test should be upstreamed or retained in the customer's CI. TrainCapsule earns recurring value only when continued environment materialization, private references, cross-stack execution, topology/hardware access, recovery assurance, or evidence operations remain necessary.

### Exchange

When an external organization adds value, TrainCapsule creates a provider-, framework-, or hardware-native evidence package or executes a federated experiment inside that organization's boundary.

Exchange is useful for:

- upstream regressions;
- provider support;
- workload-specific device investigations;
- RMA or quarantine evidence;
- service-credit evidence inventories;
- and shared technical dispositions.

External acceptance is **not** required for the first customer to receive value. A two-sided standard is optional upside, not a dependency built into the initial commercial transaction.

## 00.2 Why this is a stronger bet than the earlier incident-only shape

The incident-only product had five structural weaknesses:

1. expensive incidents may be too rare for recurring contracts;
2. automatic recovery may resolve enough events that customers rationally skip deep closure;
3. native platforms can bundle detection, remediation, and support at an apparent incremental price near zero;
4. a successful reproduction often becomes one upstream test, removing future paid work for that exact defect;
5. first-divergence and reduced-scale execution are increasingly becoming independent research and platform capabilities.

The revised product corrects each weakness:

| Structural risk | Final correction |
|---|---|
| Rare incident trigger | Qualify runs failure-derived contracts across real stack changes. |
| Customer prefers restart | Run `ClosureValueQualification` first and stop when deep closure is uneconomic. |
| Native tools improve | Import and benchmark them; duplicate primitives are replaceable backends. |
| Bug becomes free test | Upstream ordinary assertions; monetize private cross-stack execution and recovery assurance only. |
| Research absorbs a primitive | Use backend interfaces for operator alignment, scale emulation, hardware dependence, and checkpoint state. |
| External party will not adopt | Make the customer-local result sufficient by itself. |
| Exact reproduction impossible | Support structural, statistical, source/substitute, and evidence-only tiers with explicit applicability envelopes. |
| Customer will not export code/data | Default to federated execution and signed bounded results. |
| AI loop builds the wrong wedge | Maintain a Wedge Discovery Ledger and force periodic `KEEP`, `INTEGRATE`, `UPSTREAM`, `NARROW`, `REPLACE`, or `STOP` decisions. |

## 00.3 The most defensible company boundary

TrainCapsule does **not** defend:

- the recorder;
- a log parser;
- an event graph;
- an LLM explanation;
- one first-divergence algorithm;
- one reduction algorithm;
- one replay engine;
- one provider ticket template;
- or one synthetic benchmark.

Those can be copied, upstreamed, or bundled.

The defensible boundary is the complete trusted operating system for failure-derived workload contracts:

```text
customer-local evidence and identity
+ controlled experiment planning
+ replaceable diagnosis/emulation backends
+ faithfulness and applicability contracts
+ recovery-state assurance
+ private/federated execution
+ contract drift, expiry, and requalification
+ provider/upstream-native handoffs
+ longitudinal operational trust
```

The base business must work without a cross-customer data moat or industry-standard adoption. Those are upside only.

## 00.4 Replaceable backend law

TrainCapsule must assume that frameworks, clouds, vendors, and research systems will continue to absorb technical primitives.

The stable product interfaces are:

- `CollectiveTraceBackend`
- `OperatorAlignmentBackend`
- `ScaleEmulationBackend`
- `HardwareDependenceBackend`
- `CheckpointStateBackend`
- `EnvironmentMaterializationBackend`
- `SupportExportBackend`

Examples:

- PyTorch Flight Recorder can supply collective and call-stack evidence. [S01]
- An OpGuard-compatible backend can provide operator-boundary alignment for numerical/correctness investigations. [S29]
- A PrismLLM-compatible backend can provide scale-emulation capabilities when production-scale behavior cannot be reproduced by naive downscaling, subject to workload-specific validation. [S30]
- Provider- or vendor-native health systems can supply device and infrastructure evidence.

TrainCapsule competes only when its completed workflow produces material incremental value above the best available backend combination.

## 00.5 Initial wedge

The first released pack is:

> **`PRE_COLLECTIVE_LIFECYCLE_CONTRACT_V1` — pre-collective control-flow and distributed-lifecycle failure reproduction.**

It covers a bounded family in which actors cease to satisfy a declared collective or lifecycle contract because of an upstream event such as:

- omitted, reordered, or incompatible collectives;
- a data-dependent control-flow branch;
- a data-loader or checkpoint path preventing progress;
- process exit or illegal group membership change;
- or a bounded runtime/kernel lifecycle condition that can be separated from ordinary delay.

Flight Recorder is a mandatory baseline and evidence input. TrainCapsule receives no credit for merely identifying the missing rank, collective, shape mismatch, or source line already produced by native tools.

The pack succeeds commercially only when it additionally:

- preserves or reconstructs the upstream trigger;
- reduces the execution burden materially;
- produces a faithful executable or federated experiment;
- distinguishes omission, delay, process failure, and infrastructure failure;
- evaluates the recovery/guard;
- and creates a durable contract or native support artifact.

The second planned pack is:

> **`CHECKPOINT_RESUME_STATE_CONSISTENCY_V1`**

This was selected because increasingly effective restart and checkpointless recovery make post-recovery state assurance more—not less—important. It evaluates checkpoint integrity, optimizer/scheduler state, RNG and sampler continuity, data cursor, shard ownership, short-run trajectory sentinels, throughput, and declared observation windows. It does not claim universal long-horizon model correctness.

Further packs are evidence-selected, not precommitted:

- numerical divergence through an operator-alignment backend;
- workload-specific hardware dependence with a provider/hardware partner;
- fail-slow only when native tools identify the slow component but do not reproduce or close it;
- heterogeneous post-training actor graphs only after the initial IR proves extensible.

## 00.8 Customer and buyer

The best initial customer is a workload platform or model organization that:

- runs repeated distributed PyTorch/NCCL workloads;
- controls launch, images, checkpoints, and local experiment execution;
- has experienced at least one materially costly unresolved failure or has high-consequence stack changes;
- already uses native diagnostics, creating a fair benchmark;
- has a small enough systems team that incident work competes with core roadmap work;
- can supply a second workload, incident, or qualification trigger;
- and has a named budget owner.

A managed training provider or neocloud is a strong secondary segment when it has enough support volume but does not already possess the complete workflow.

Frontier labs and hyperscalers are strategically attractive but poor assumptions for an initial sale because internal capability and security boundaries are strongest.

Accounts are disqualified when:

- native mitigation is sufficient and residual uncertainty is acceptable;
- the customer lacks evidence or experiment authority;
- the product would require prohibited source, data, or checkpoint export;
- the incident value cannot exceed integration and delivery cost;
- or the customer demands guaranteed root cause, hardware certification, or universal recovery safety.

## 00.10 What would make the product a real business

The company thesis becomes supported only when the following are observed:

1. **Complete-substitute advantage:** TrainCapsule materially improves on the buyer's framework tools, cloud/platform features, vendor support, internal engineers, and approved coding/operations agents taken together.
2. **One-party value:** the purchaser benefits even if no external provider accepts a TrainCapsule format.
3. **Faithful cost reduction:** a real incident becomes materially cheaper, faster, or more accessible without changing the relevant mechanism.
4. **Bounded recovery value:** named state properties change a real recovery or release decision.
5. **Contract value:** at least some confirmed failures retain value through future stack qualification after ordinary tests are upstreamed.
6. **Repeat use:** a useful first result causes a second incident, qualification, protected workload, or paid continuation.
7. **Productization:** later same-pack cases use the stable core and declarative packs rather than trust-core rewrites and hidden founder reasoning.
8. **Security feasibility:** local/federated execution satisfies customer policy.
9. **Reachable market:** enough named accounts have pain, authority, budget, and insufficient substitutes.
10. **Delivery economics:** adapter maintenance, GPU experiments, security review, and senior engineering leave an acceptable margin.

## 00.13 Hard stop and pivot conditions

The loop must narrow, replace, or stop the commercial thesis when any of these persist after a bounded remediation cycle:

- the best native/bundled/agent workflow produces the same complete outcome;
- representative incidents cannot be reduced or investigated faithfully;
- reduced results disagree with production-like sentinels;
- instrumentation materially changes the incident;

- customers consistently prefer mitigation and accept residual uncertainty;
- exact or equivalent environments are unavailable or uneconomic;
- ordinary upstream changes repeatedly require trust-core rewrites;
- most useful contracts become ordinary tests with no remaining paid operation;
- no second commercial action follows a material result;
- delivery remains dominated by unique senior investigation labor at poor margins;
- a false confirmed hardware or component claim occurs;
- security containment fails;
- the reachable customer-controlled segment is too small and providers will not integrate;
- or acquisition framing begins to drive features that do not improve customer value.

# 01 — External Reality and Market Direction

## 01.1 Native recovery and diagnosis are expanding quickly

The strongest current threat is not that distributed workloads become failure-free. It is that first-party systems make ordinary failures cheap enough that a separate product loses its initial wedge.

Current public capabilities include:

- PyTorch Flight Recorder automatically dumps critical c10d evidence after NCCL watchdog timeouts and performs cross-rank analysis; current releases have broadened backend and collective coverage. [S01][S05]
- TorchFT provides per-step worker health, reconfigurable process groups, and live state recovery from healthy peers for fault-tolerant DDP/HSDP patterns. [S02]
- NVIDIA Mission Control Autonomous Job Recovery detects, isolates, attributes, and recovers many job and hardware failures in supported DGX environments. [S03][S06]
- SageMaker HyperPod supports automatic node recovery, auto-resume, checkpointless training, in-process restart, and peer-to-peer in-memory state recovery for supported stacks. [S04][S07]

**Reasoned inference:** routine node failures, process failures, and known communication failures will increasingly become platform features rather than standalone startup opportunities.

**Product consequence:** TrainCapsule must benchmark against the customer’s current native recovery and diagnosis stack. “We found a missing collective” or “the job restarted” is not sufficient differentiation.

## 01.2 Native tools remain incomplete and constrained

The same documentation also shows boundaries:

- recovery features are tied to supported frameworks, versions, orchestration modes, and workload structures;
- some resilience features are mutually incompatible or have prerequisites;
- recovery can restore execution without proving why the incident occurred or whether application state remained valid;
- exact diagnosis often still requires workload-specific evidence;
- and public documentation cannot establish the full capability of internal provider systems. [S04][S07][S08]

**Reasoned inference:** the residual opportunity is not “recovery is missing.” It is “recovery and native diagnosis do not always establish a faithful failure mechanism, state assurance, or durable regression contract.”

## 01.3 The workload center is broadening

Training is not disappearing, but the operational unit is becoming broader than one synchronous pre-training loop.

Current public systems combine:

- pre-training, post-training, and inference phases in the same AI-factory strategy; [S09]
- rollout engines, distributed trainers, Ray orchestration, NCCL/RDMA weight synchronization, MoE routing, and fault tolerance in large-scale reinforcement-learning post-training; [S10]
- multiple parallelism dimensions including data, tensor, pipeline, context, and expert parallelism; [S11]
- NVIDIA and AMD accelerator ecosystems, with PyTorch distributed runtimes expanding across both. [S12]

**Reasoned inference:** a product hard-coded around DDP/FSDP rank traces alone can become obsolete even while accelerated-workload incidents grow. The architecture needs a general actor/workload graph, while the first implementation remains narrow.

## 01.4 Reproducibility is bounded, not universal

PyTorch explicitly states that complete reproducibility is not guaranteed across releases, individual commits, or platforms. CUDA applications and containers also remain dependent on compatible drivers, toolkits, hardware, and feature support. [S13][S14]

**Product consequence:** every reproducer and incident contract needs:

- an applicability envelope;
- exact dependency and environment identity;
- a declared replay tier;
- an expiration/revalidation policy;
- and an explicit state when the original environment is no longer reconstructable.

No artifact may be marketed as indefinitely executable.

## 01.5 Production-scale observability already exists

Research and production reports demonstrate that sophisticated operators can build low-overhead, cross-layer diagnosis internally:

- ARGUS reports always-on tracing across CPU, framework, and GPU-kernel layers at more than 10,000 GPUs with combined overhead below 2%; [S15]
- SysOM-AI reports deployment across more than 80,000 GPUs, less than 0.4% overhead, and substantial diagnosis-time reductions; [S16]
- SDCHUNTER reports exact-workload replay for workload-specific silent data corruption and 40 mitigated incidents. [S17]

These results establish technical feasibility within the studied organizations. They also establish a serious competitive threat: the largest-pain customers often possess the strongest internal build capacity.

## 01.6 The market is a barbell

The initial market has a structural barbell:

```text
largest labs and hyperscalers
    very high pain
    very high internal capability and strict data boundaries

small teams
    low internal capability
    often lower incident frequency, scale, or budget

best initial segment
    enough repeated pain to justify purchase
    insufficient internal breadth to build the entire workflow
```

The first commercial segment should therefore not be “all organizations using AI” or automatically “the largest frontier labs.” It should be a training platform, model company, research platform, or AI infrastructure operator that:

- runs repeated distributed workloads;
- has measurable costly incidents;
- controls enough of the execution boundary to deploy locally;
- supports several teams, workloads, or clouds;
- has a small enough systems team that closing incidents competes with core roadmap work;
- and can make a purchase without requiring an unaffiliated provider to adopt a new standard.

## 01.7 Market-size discipline

Broad AI adoption is not a valid TrainCapsule market-size proxy. Organizational AI use includes API consumption, SaaS, inference-only use, and small fine-tunes. Stanford’s 2026 AI Index reports strong industry participation and broad adoption, but it does not enumerate organizations running recurring distributed training or post-training workloads that fit this product. [S18]

Use bottom-up account economics:

```text
qualified account
× qualifying incidents or protected stack changes per year
× recoverable operational value
× obtainable price
× expected retention
```

The market model must use named accounts and direct discovery evidence. Do not publish a total-addressable-market number derived from general AI spending.

## 01.8 Bundling economics is a stronger threat than simple feature overlap

Current cloud and infrastructure products increasingly bundle reliability, telemetry, remediation, and expert support into the compute relationship:

- CoreWeave states that Mission Control is included with CoreWeave Cloud and publicly offers cluster observability, rank/GPU/node straggler detection, lifecycle health management, an interactive agent, proactive remediation paths, and direct-to-expert support. [S25]
- Crusoe AutoClusters continuously monitors supported hardware issues and can drain, restart or replace nodes, reschedule workloads, expose remediation history, and integrate external decision logic through webhooks and APIs. [S26]
- Chamber publicly markets cross-cloud monitoring, root-cause analysis, configuration repair, checkpoint rerun, and incident replay. [S27]
- Caladrius publicly markets diagnosis, remediation, and verification across device, fabric, storage, and workload layers. [S28]

These are public product contracts and vendor claims, not independent proof of performance. They nevertheless establish the economic threat:

> **The customer may perceive the native alternative as already paid for through cloud spend, premium support, or an existing platform contract.**

The relevant competitor is therefore not only another startup. It is:

```text
bundled platform capability
+ existing support relationship
+ privileged telemetry
+ no new vendor approval
+ low switching cost
```

**Product consequence:** TrainCapsule must demonstrate incremental value against the customer's bundled provider workflow, not against raw logs or no tooling. The strongest surviving boundaries are customer-controlled private evidence, cross-provider continuity, faithful mechanism experiments, bounded recovery-state assurance, and failure-derived contracts that remain useful through stack changes.

# 02 — Fatal-Flaw Audit and Mandatory Corrections

## 02.1 Fatal flaw: cross-company acceptance deadlock

### Why it can destroy the business

A product cannot make an external provider, framework maintainer, or hardware vendor accept evidence. Those parties operate their own ticket formats and diagnostic collectors. AWS, NVIDIA, and other infrastructure vendors already ship native support-bundle tooling and may ask customers to submit those artifacts. [S19][S20]

Requiring an external signed receipt creates:

- a two-sided sales motion;
- integration work before value;
- dependence on a party that does not pay;
- and possible conflict when the evidence supports customer credits, RMA, or provider fault.

### Correction

- The minimum commercial outcome is internal to the purchasing organization.
- External exchange uses **provider-native exports first**.
- A generic `DispositionRecord` may record what an external party did, but no external signature is required for product success.
- Provider-side adoption is a later channel, not the initial proof.

### Kill test

If customers receive no material value unless an external organization adopts a TrainCapsule-specific protocol, stop the broad company thesis.

## 02.2 Fatal flaw: first observed divergence can be mistaken for root cause

### Why it can destroy trust

Instrumentation observes only selected boundaries. The earliest recorded difference may be downstream of an unobserved data, scheduling, memory, compiler, or hardware event. A violated collective contract can identify where ranks ceased to agree without proving why they diverged.

### Correction

Use separate records:

```text
SymptomRecord
ObservedDivergenceRecord
CausalPrecursorRecord
MechanismExperimentRecord
ComponentDispositionRecord
OperationalOwnerRecord
```

Only a controlled experiment that changes the outcome while preserving declared controls may support a causal mechanism claim. Otherwise the system reports a boundary, competing hypotheses, and the next experiment.

### Kill test

Any design that maps “first event we observed” directly to “party responsible” is blocked from external release.

## 02.3 Fatal flaw: “smallest reproducer” is an unjustified promise

### Why it can destroy technical credibility

Reduction across ranks, steps, data, model structure, topology, software, and hardware is combinatorial. A bounded system normally cannot prove global minimality.

### Correction

The product produces a **Minimum Faithful Experiment within Budget**, defined as the lowest-cost accepted candidate found under:

- a declared search space;
- a resource and wall-clock budget;
- a faithfulness predicate;
- a privacy constraint;
- and a stopped-search reason.

Use “smaller,” “lower-cost,” or “best found within budget.” Use “minimal” only when the search space and proof establish it.

### Kill test

If the resulting experiment remains nearly as costly, private, or operationally inaccessible as the original incident, the reduction does not create product value.

## 02.4 Fatal flaw: executable capsules may be unsafe or impossible to share

### Why it can destroy deployment

A reproducer may contain customer source, model architecture, checkpoints, data relationships, topology, credentials, generated kernels, or exploit-capable code. The receiving party may refuse arbitrary execution. Sanitization can remove the exact trigger.

### Correction

Default to **federated verification**:

1. TrainCapsule sends a signed experiment plan to a customer- or provider-controlled runner.
2. The runner verifies policy and materializes approved local references.
3. The experiment executes inside the owning boundary.
4. Only signed results, digests, bounded evidence, and an attestation leave.
5. A portable executable reproducer is produced only when data rights, security, and faithfulness permit it.

Confidential GPU containers and remote attestation can strengthen selected deployments, but current support has hardware and topology constraints and therefore cannot be assumed universally. [S21][S22]

### Kill test

If the product requires prohibited source, weights, or data to leave the customer environment, redesign or disqualify that case.

## 02.5 Fatal flaw: instrumentation can change the failure

### Why it can destroy validity

Distributed failures can be timing-sensitive. Instrumentation changes CPU work, memory pressure, synchronization, I/O, and event ordering. PyTorch’s own Flight Recorder work favors offline analysis and notes performance/scaling problems in older debug approaches. Production tracing papers also treat overhead as a central design constraint. [S01][S15]

### Correction

Every capture path receives a perturbation profile:

- randomized capture-on/capture-off trials;
- full latency and step-time distributions, not only the mean;
- memory, storage, CPU, and network impact;
- collective wait and timeout distribution;
- incident-rate difference where enough trials exist;
- recorder failure behavior;
- and exact recorder identity in the workload lock.

Start import-only. Add proprietary instrumentation only when native evidence is insufficient and its value exceeds perturbation risk.

### Kill test

If the supported capture mode materially changes the incident predicate or produces recurrent false incidents, it cannot be armed on production workloads.

## 02.6 Fatal flaw: a green reduced test can create false assurance

### Why it can destroy customer outcomes

A reduced two-GPU case may omit topology, scale, contention, parallelism, data distribution, and timing conditions required by the original failure. Passing that case after a fix does not prove the full workload is safe.

### Correction

Every incident contract has an **Applicability Envelope**:

- supported framework and component versions;
- hardware and topology class;
- parallelism graph;
- scale range;
- data/checkpoint/RNG assumptions;
- concurrency and load assumptions;
- required private references;
- and expiration conditions.

Recovery validation is tiered:

```text
REDUCED_EXPERIMENT_PASSED
TOPOLOGY_PRESERVING_CANARY_PASSED
PRODUCTION_LIKE_CANARY_PASSED
OBSERVATION_WINDOW_PASSED
NOT_EVALUATED
UNKNOWN
```

The product must periodically run a higher-fidelity sentinel when the original mechanism depends on scale or topology.

### Kill test

If reduced tests repeatedly pass while production-like canaries reproduce the original incident, the pack’s faithfulness model is invalid and release is blocked.

## 02.7 Fatal flaw: replay artifacts decay

### Why it can destroy recurring value

Frameworks, drivers, kernels, containers, registries, firmware, and hardware availability change. PyTorch does not promise exact reproducibility across releases or platforms. [S13]

### Correction

Every artifact has:

- `created_at`;
- `last_verified_at`;
- `valid_through` or a revalidation policy;
- source and target environment identities;
- dependency and image retention state;
- hardware availability state;
- and one of `CURRENT`, `STALE`, `EXPIRED`, `UNAVAILABLE`, or `REVOKED`.

Artifact preservation is a paid operational feature only when customers value it; it is not an eternal guarantee.

### Kill test

Do not sell recurring “protection” when most incident contracts cannot be re-executed or meaningfully mapped after normal stack changes.

## 02.8 Fatal flaw: native tools can absorb the first pack

### Why it can destroy differentiation

Flight Recorder already detects missing collectives, cross-rank mismatches, and associated call sites; PyTorch continues expanding its trace analyzer. [S01][S05]

### Correction

The first pack is not “NCCL hang detection.” It is:

> **Pre-collective control-flow and lifecycle contract reproduction**

Commercial value requires at least one outcome beyond native analysis:

- preserve the data/control-flow trigger;
- lower execution cost materially;
- generate a faithful standalone or federated experiment;
- distinguish a delayed operation from an omitted operation;
- verify a guard or recovery state;
- or convert the case into a durable private/upstream contract.

Benchmark against the latest stable Flight Recorder and customer support process before every external claim.

### Kill test

If native tools produce the same actionable experiment and recovery result with comparable effort, upstream useful work and stop selling that pack.

## 02.9 Fatal flaw: modern workloads exceed the initial rank model

### Why it can destroy the roadmap

Current large workloads combine tensor, pipeline, context, expert, and data parallelism; RL post-training composes rollout, training, orchestration, reward, data transfer, and weight synchronization. [S10][S11]

### Correction

The core incident representation uses actors, resources, phases, state channels, and semantic contracts rather than assuming one flat DDP rank set. The first released adapter supports a narrow c10d collective graph. Later adapters may describe:

- trainer and rollout actor groups;
- pipeline stages;
- expert-routing groups;
- checkpoint and weight-transfer channels;
- inference/training policy synchronization;
- and accelerator-vendor-specific communication backends.

Do not build these before qualified incidents demand them.

### Kill test

If more than a material share of qualified prospects are blocked by unsupported workload structure and the architecture cannot add it through adapters/packs, redesign the core IR before scaling sales.

## 02.10 Fatal flaw: provider-first can select the wrong payer

### Why it can destroy sales

A provider has incident volume, but also internal tooling, liability concerns, and incentives to control fault narratives. A workload operator has stronger motivation for portable evidence but fewer incidents.

### Correction

Use a buyer hierarchy rather than a mandatory provider-first rule:

1. **Workload platform or model operator with direct internal ROI and repeated jobs.**
2. **Managed training provider or neocloud with support burden and a limited internal reliability team.**
3. **Accelerator or framework organization for specific enablement workflows.**
4. **Hyperscaler or frontier lab only after independent proof.**

Choose the first payer based on access, incident recurrence, authority to deploy, and measurable cost—not on strategic branding.

### Kill test

Do not pursue an account that requires three organizations to sign before one bounded pilot can run.

## 02.11 Fatal flaw: annual readiness can be perceived as insurance

### Why it can destroy recurring revenue

Pre-incident readiness has counterfactual value and may be difficult to budget before a customer experiences a successful closure.

### Correction

Commercial sequencing is:

```text
paid historical or active-incident assessment
→ bounded current incident engagement
→ demonstrated useful result
→ incident contract and stack-change qualification
→ annual critical-workload assurance
```

Do not assume customers will buy annual protection cold. Earn the recurring contract after an incident or readiness drill demonstrates concrete value.

### Kill test

If annual contracts are not requested after useful closures, keep a per-incident or retainer service and do not build SaaS economics around imagined subscriptions.

## 02.12 Fatal flaw: data and standard moats may never exist

### Why it can destroy defensibility claims

Customers may permit only local execution and deletion. Providers may not adopt a new format. A standard used by one company is not a standard.

### Correction

The base business must work with `P0` or `P1` data rights:

- no cross-customer data;
- no public capsule;
- no shared signature;
- and no provider-specific adoption.

The base moat is local workflow integration, incident history, verified contracts, pack quality, security trust, and operating expertise. Permissioned aggregate knowledge and an open protocol are optional upside.

### Kill test

Do not use “network effect,” “industry standard,” or “data moat” in company claims until independent organizations create or consume artifacts without founder intervention.

## 02.13 Fatal flaw: arbitrary reuse percentages can hide the real economics

### Why it can destroy a viable path

A 95% code-reuse target can be gamed or can reject a profitable software-assisted expert service. Code reuse does not measure manual interpretation, security review, GPU cost, or customer integration.

### Correction

Track separate productization metrics:

- trust-core code changed;
- customer-specific executable code;
- new adapter/pack work;
- senior investigation hours;
- founder-only hours;
- deployment time;
- time to first useful evidence;
- direct GPU/cloud cost;
- gross margin;
- and operator independence.

A productized service is an acceptable business if it has bounded scope, strong margin, recurring demand, and compounding tooling. Do not falsely call it SaaS.

### Kill test

If revenue grows only by adding senior incident engineers and customer-specific code remains dominant, classify the company as a specialist service and choose deliberately whether that outcome meets the founder’s goals.

## 02.14 Fatal flaw: recovery verification can overclaim model correctness

### Why it can destroy trust

A resumed job can pass a short canary while optimizer state, sampler position, RNG, or long-horizon training trajectory remains wrong. Numerical differences can amplify in iterative training and post-training workflows.

### Correction

Use **Recovery Assurance**, not unconditional recovery certification. Evaluate named properties:

- checkpoint integrity and completeness;
- optimizer and scheduler state;
- RNG and sampler state;
- data cursor and replay/skip policy;
- distributed membership and shard ownership;
- loss/gradient/tensor sentinels;
- throughput and resource behavior;
- short-run trajectory comparison;
- and declared observation window.

Unmeasured long-horizon model quality remains `UNKNOWN`.

### Kill test

Any public statement that a recovery is “safe” without naming tested properties and limitations is prohibited.

## 02.15 Fatal flaw: successful-case selection can inflate ROI

### Why it can destroy business decisions

Incident programs naturally emphasize dramatic wins while unsupported, `UNKNOWN`, or no-gain cases disappear. Estimated historical baselines can overstate savings.

### Correction

Maintain an immutable case ledger containing:

- accepted and rejected incidents;
- all failed experiments;
- `UNKNOWN` results;
- product integration cost;
- customer and founder effort;
- native-tool baseline;
- negative operational effects;
- and the confidence of every economic input.

Use pre-registered thresholds and independent customer approval for value claims. Prefer blinded parallel investigation, matched historical incidents, or measured current workflows over retrospective estimates.

### Kill test

If the company cannot retain an auditable denominator of attempted cases, do not publish success rates or average savings.

## 02.16 Fatal flaw: bundled cloud tooling can reduce the standalone price to approximately zero

### Why it can destroy the business

Even a technically superior tool may lose when the customer's current cloud or platform already includes telemetry, remediation, support, and workflow integration. CoreWeave explicitly states that Mission Control is included with its cloud offering, while other providers and vendors expose automated remediation and full-loop operations surfaces. [S25][S26][S27][S28]

The customer may rationally prefer an adequate bundled workflow over a better standalone product that adds:

- security review;
- procurement;
- deployment and support burden;
- another incident database;
- and uncertainty about startup continuity.

### Correction

Every commercial benchmark must include:

```text
latest native framework tools
+ bundled cloud/platform reliability features
+ contracted support
+ internal expert workflow
+ approved coding/operations agents
```

TrainCapsule may charge only for a material incremental outcome, such as:

- customer-controlled evidence that survives cloud migration;
- faithful reproduction unavailable from provider telemetry;
- private cross-stack experiments the provider cannot run;
- recovery-state assurance beyond restart;
- or maintained incident contracts across customer-selected stack changes.

Do not monetize a dashboard, rank finder, node replacement, or support-bundle wrapper already included with the customer's platform.

### Kill test

If qualified accounts consistently judge the bundled workflow sufficient and will not commit money, hardware, or engineering time for the incremental outcome, stop the standalone software thesis for that segment.

## 02.17 Fatal flaw: a successful incident contract can collapse into one free repository test

### Why it can destroy recurring revenue

The better TrainCapsule performs, the more likely a deterministic software defect becomes a small regression test or upstream fix. That is a successful technical outcome but can remove future paid work for that exact incident.

### Correction

Classify every confirmed incident after closure:

```text
UPSTREAMABLE_REPOSITORY_TEST
PRIVATE_CROSS_STACK_CONTRACT
ENVIRONMENT_OR_TOPOLOGY_QUALIFICATION
HARDWARE_OR_FLEET_QUALIFICATION
EVIDENCE_ONLY_OPERATIONAL_RECORD
NO_DURABLE_CONTRACT
```

Rules:

- `UPSTREAMABLE_REPOSITORY_TEST`: contribute or hand off the minimal test when rights permit. Do not charge indefinitely for storing the same test.
- `PRIVATE_CROSS_STACK_CONTRACT`: recurring value is allowed only when private references, multiple components, environment materialization, or recovery-state checks must be maintained.
- `ENVIRONMENT_OR_TOPOLOGY_QUALIFICATION`: recurring value comes from executing the contract against real stack changes, not from owning the assertion text.
- `HARDWARE_OR_FLEET_QUALIFICATION`: recurring value requires controlled devices, topology, evidence, and operational disposition.
- `NO_DURABLE_CONTRACT`: close the case and do not force it into an annual product.

The Incident Contract Registry may contain only:

- contracts derived from confirmed incidents;
- customer-approved recovery-state controls;
- or explicitly purchased readiness controls.

It must not expand into a generic compatibility matrix merely to create recurring usage.

### Kill test

If useful cases mostly become ordinary repository tests and customers do not value environment execution, recovery assurance, or contract operations afterward, retain the open-source/testing value and stop the annual-assurance thesis.

## 02.18 Fatal flaw: exact replay environments and spare capacity may be unavailable or uneconomic

### Why it can destroy the core transaction

The source GPU model, driver, firmware, network topology, container registry, checkpoint, or data reference may no longer exist. During an active incident, the customer may have no spare accelerators and may prioritize production recovery over reproduction. Owning a broad historical GPU fleet would create capital and utilization risk for a small company.

### Correction

Every experiment plan includes an `EnvironmentAvailabilityRecord`:

```text
SOURCE_EXACT
SOURCE_EQUIVALENT_WITH_DECLARED_DIFFERENCES
SOURCE_ADJACENT
STRUCTURAL_SUBSTITUTE
CUSTOMER_LOCAL_ONLY
UNAVAILABLE
```

Operating rules:

- customer or provider capacity is the default source lane;
- rented capacity is used only under a pre-agreed resource budget;
- TrainCapsule does not purchase a broad GPU fleet before recurring utilization is proven;
- source-exact, topology-preserving, and structural experiments are separate claims;
- unavailable firmware/hardware becomes an explicit limitation;
- production capacity cannot be consumed without customer approval and workload priority controls;
- the planner aborts when expected experiment cost or elapsed time exceeds the customer's decision value.

### Kill test

If representative supported incidents routinely require unavailable environments or direct experiment cost destroys customer surplus and gross margin, the reproduction product is not viable for that pack.

## 02.19 Fatal flaw: adapter and version churn can consume the entire margin

### Why it can destroy productization

PyTorch, CUDA, NCCL, drivers, schedulers, and cloud APIs continue to change. A large adapter surface can make every release a compatibility project and turn recurring revenue into maintenance labor.

### Correction

Adopt a strict support-lane policy:

- one released framework/runtime lane first;
- current and one prior supported lane only after demand;
- exact adapter conformance fixtures and loss declarations;
- semantic versioning for evidence contracts;
- announced deprecation and end-of-support dates;
- no private version lane without a paying customer;
- trust-core changes forbidden for ordinary adapter drift;
- maintenance hours, breakage rate, and revenue tracked by lane.

A new framework, scheduler, accelerator, or cloud is a separate product-surface decision, not a sales checkbox.

### Kill test

If ordinary upstream releases repeatedly require trust-core rewrites, break incident contracts faster than customers use them, or make adapter maintenance exceed the revenue of the supported lane, deprecate the lane or stop expansion.

## 02.20 Fatal flaw: incident urgency and enterprise procurement work against each other

### Why it can destroy customer acquisition

An active incident creates urgency, but a new vendor may not clear security, legal, procurement, and deployment review before the incident is mitigated. Before an incident, the same buyer may not prioritize a tool whose value is counterfactual.

### Correction

Use a low-risk entry path:

1. open or signed local importer/verifier with no production control;
2. paid historical incident or evidence-readiness assessment;
3. customer-local appliance with no external telemetry;
4. pre-approved bounded current-incident engagement;
5. recurring assurance only after observed value.

Continuity controls:

- the product is fail-open and not required for the training job to proceed;
- customer data and artifacts remain exportable in documented formats;
- offline verifier and contract runner remain available to the customer;
- no 24/7 emergency SLA is sold before staffing, escalation, insurance, and incident response support it;
- source escrow or equivalent continuity terms are considered only when a qualified buyer requires them;
- support scope and response windows are contractual, not implied.

### Kill test

If qualified buyers cannot pre-authorize the local product and active incidents always resolve before onboarding, use a channel/OEM/service model or stop direct enterprise sales.

## 02.21 Fatal flaw: a permanent evidence record can create legal and organizational resistance

### Why it can destroy adoption

An incident record may affect contractual fault allocation, service credits, warranties, employment review, regulatory inquiries, or litigation. A technical artifact does not determine legal responsibility, and customers or providers may avoid a system that appears to adjudicate blame.

### Correction

- Use `ComponentDispositionRecord`, not legal-fault or liability verdict.
- Separate technical mechanism, operational owner, and contractual responsibility.
- Customer policy controls retention, access, legal hold, deletion, and export.
- Do not promise attorney-client privilege or work-product protection; qualified counsel must define any such process.
- No automatic service-credit, warranty, personnel, or disciplinary decision.
- External reports disclose sponsor, evidence limits, untested alternatives, and dispute status.
- Public naming of a provider, employee, or device requires explicit authorization and evidence review.

### Kill test

If the target buyer's legal and operational policies prevent creation of even a local bounded technical record, disqualify the account rather than weakening provenance or truth rules.

## 02.22 Fatal flaw: customers may rationally prefer mitigation over closure

### Why it can destroy demand

When native recovery restarts a job and the incident is unlikely to recur, a deep causal investigation may cost more than it saves. Better recovery can therefore shrink the set of incidents worth reproducing even when failures continue. [S02][S03][S04][S26]

### Correction

Add an early `ClosureValueQualification` before expensive analysis:

- recurrence or credible recurrence risk;
- corruption or state-integrity risk;
- material compute or schedule cost;
- repeated support handoff;
- unresolved component decision;
- blocked upgrade, hardware return, or release;
- and value of a durable contract.

Terminal outcomes include:

```text
MITIGATE_AND_CLOSE_WITHOUT_DEEP_REPRODUCTION
PROCEED_TO_BOUNDED_EXPERIMENT
ESCALATE_EVIDENCE_ONLY
UNSUPPORTED_OR_UNECONOMIC
```

The product should sometimes advise the customer not to spend more.

### Kill test

If qualified operators consistently accept native mitigation and do not value recurrence prevention, state assurance, or a bounded mechanism record, narrow to the rare high-consequence segment or stop the company thesis.

## 02.23 Fatal flaw: AI operations agents can commoditize narrative diagnosis and simple repair

### Why it can destroy differentiation

Current products already market conversational diagnosis, autonomous configuration changes, checkpoint reruns, and full-loop remediation. [S25][S27][S28] General coding and operations agents will likely improve the speed of log interpretation and script generation; the degree and timing are uncertain.

### Correction

TrainCapsule must not defend itself through prose quality or generic experiment suggestions. Its authority must remain:

- immutable execution identity;
- declared evidence completeness and perturbation;
- controlled-variable experiments;
- faithfulness and applicability contracts;
- recovery-state checks;
- security policy;
- and auditable positive, negative, and `UNKNOWN` results.

The native baseline includes an approved expert using available coding/operations agents. AI may propose an experiment, but the result is accepted only through execution and the declared oracle.

### Kill test

If an approved native/agent workflow produces the same faithful experiment, recovery assurance, and durable contract with comparable effort, upstream useful components and stop selling the redundant layer.

## 02.24 Fatal flaw: a shift toward managed AI factories can remove the customer's authority to deploy

### Why it can destroy the reachable market

A customer using a vertically integrated managed training platform may not control node telemetry, firmware, scheduler internals, or low-level recovery. The provider owns both the evidence and the bundled reliability system. This can increase total industry compute while shrinking the independently reachable customer market.

### Correction

Maintain two distinct routes:

```text
CUSTOMER_CONTROLLED_ROUTE
  private/on-prem/multi-cloud workload owner with local deployment authority

PROVIDER_INTEGRATED_ROUTE
  OEM, support integration, or provider-operated evidence gateway
```

Do not sell the customer-controlled product to an account that lacks the necessary evidence and execution authority. Track the reachable account count, not general AI compute growth. If the reachable market shifts toward managed platforms, partner with a provider only after the one-party product is independently proven.

### Kill test

If customer-controlled qualified accounts are too few and providers neither buy nor integrate, stop the broad commercial effort even if the technical problem remains important.

# 03 — Product Strategy and Requirements

- **Document date:** 9 August 2026
- **Status:** **Final source of truth**
- **Product category:** **Accelerated Workload Failure Reproduction and Change Qualification**
- **Initial wedge:** `PRE_COLLECTIVE_LIFECYCLE_CONTRACT_V1`
- **Second planned wedge:** `CHECKPOINT_RESUME_STATE_CONSISTENCY_V1`

## 03.1 Product definition

> **TrainCapsule is a customer-local system that converts unresolved distributed accelerator failures into bounded experiments and turns confirmed failures into expiring workload incident contracts used to qualify future software, hardware, topology, checkpoint, and cloud changes.**

TrainCapsule has three products that share one trust core:

```text
TrainCapsule Close
  unresolved failure → faithful experiment → recovery assurance → incident contract

TrainCapsule Qualify
  incident/critical-workload contract → stack or infrastructure change → bounded release decision

TrainCapsule Exchange
  local/federated evidence → provider/upstream/vendor-native artifact → technical disposition
```

The commercial promise is:

> **Make a difficult accelerated-workload failure cheaper to reproduce, safer to recover from, and harder to reintroduce.**

## 03.2 What the customer buys

The customer does not buy “root cause.” The customer buys a completed operational decision under explicit limits.

A successful engagement produces at least one of:

- a `MinimumFaithfulExperiment` that materially reduces required GPUs, runtime, private data, setup burden, or specialist time;
- a `MechanismRecord` establishing a reproduced mechanism within a named envelope;
- a `ComponentDispositionRecord` supporting or rejecting a component dependency without converting technical evidence into legal blame;
- a `RecoveryAssuranceRecord` covering named checkpoint, optimizer, RNG, sampler, shard, numerical, performance, and observation-window properties;
- a `WorkloadIncidentContract` that can run after future stack changes;
- a native provider/upstream/hardware export;
- or an explicit `MITIGATE_AND_CLOSE_WITHOUT_DEEP_REPRODUCTION`, `UNSUPPORTED_OR_UNECONOMIC`, or `UNKNOWN` result.

The product must be willing to recommend no further investigation when closure costs exceed decision value.

## 03.3 Non-goals

TrainCapsule is not:

- a generic GPU metrics or trace dashboard;
- a conversational AIOps assistant;
- a replacement for Flight Recorder, NCCL RAS/Inspector, DCGM, schedulers, checkpointing, or native recovery;
- a generic fault-tolerant training runtime;
- a GPU scheduler or cloud;
- an autonomous code-repair or production-remediation agent;
- a universal debugger;
- a hardware-certification authority;
- a legal fault-adjudication system;
- a generic compatibility matrix;
- a long-horizon model-quality guarantee;
- or an industry standard declared by one company.

## 03.4 Product loops

### Close

Required stages:

1. qualify whether deep closure is economically rational;
2. lock workload and environment identity;
3. import native evidence before adding instrumentation;
4. determine evidence completeness and perturbation;
5. locate the first **observed** inconsistency;
6. preserve alternative mechanisms;
7. plan controlled experiments;
8. search for the lowest-cost faithful experiment within budget;
9. evaluate recovery-state properties;
10. create an expiring incident contract;
11. export only what policy permits.

### Qualify

Qualify runs contracts against a declared change. It returns:

```text
PASS_WITHIN_ENVELOPE
FAIL_SAME_INCIDENT
FAIL_DIFFERENT_BOUNDARY
DRIFT_REQUIRES_REQUALIFICATION
PRIVATE_REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE
ENVIRONMENT_UNAVAILABLE
INFRASTRUCTURE_ERROR
STALE
EXPIRED
REVOKED
UNKNOWN
```

A pass means only that the named contract did not recur and the declared recovery/qualification properties passed in the tested envelope.

### Exchange

Exchange uses native formats first. It may produce:

- PyTorch/c10d/NCCL issue and regression artifact;
- cloud support bundle supplement;
- provider-local federated experiment;
- hardware workload-dependence report;
- quarantine/RMA evidence inventory;
- customer incident report;
- signed attestation and bounded result.

No external organization is required to accept a proprietary capsule format for the initial customer to receive value.

## 03.5 Primary customer

The primary initial segment is a model company, research platform, or internal training platform that:

- operates repeated distributed PyTorch/NCCL workloads;
- controls images, launch, checkpoints, evidence, and local experiments;
- has experienced a material unresolved failure or has high-consequence stack changes;
- has a platform/reliability owner;
- already uses native tools;
- has multiple workloads or a credible second use;
- and can fund a named incident or qualification outcome.

The second segment is a managed training platform or neocloud with enough customer incident volume and a limited internal replay/reduction workflow.

Do not assume frontier labs or hyperscalers are the first customer. Their pain is high, but internal capability and data boundaries are also highest.

### Evidence and Incident Feasibility Assessment

Delivers:

- supported/unsupported decision;
- native-tool inventory;
- workload/evidence lock readiness;
- privacy and execution tier;
- closure-value qualification;
- candidate experiment budget;

### Provider Evidence Gateway

## 03.8 Flagship incident pack

### `PRE_COLLECTIVE_LIFECYCLE_CONTRACT_V1`

Supported mechanisms:

- one actor omits a declared collective boundary;
- actors call incompatible collective types or payload contracts;
- data-dependent control flow changes collective order;
- a data-loader/checkpoint path prevents one actor from reaching the boundary;
- a process exits or illegally changes membership;
- a bounded runtime/kernel lifecycle condition prevents progress and can be separated from ordinary delay.

Required commercial outcome beyond native tooling:

- reconstruct or preserve the upstream trigger;
- materially reduce execution/access burden;
- distinguish omission, delay, lifecycle failure, and infrastructure failure;
- execute a faithful local/federated experiment;
- evaluate the guard or recovery;
- create a durable contract or upstream/native support artifact.

Explicit non-support:

- generic network health;
- generic NCCL tuning;
- broad straggler detection;
- every deadlock;
- universal kernel hang analysis;
- hardware certification;
- arbitrary pipeline/expert-parallel graphs in the first release.

## 03.9 Second pack

### `CHECKPOINT_RESUME_STATE_CONSISTENCY_V1`

This pack evaluates whether a recovery preserved declared state properties after checkpoint, checkpointless, elastic, in-process, or node-replacement recovery.

Required properties are selected per workload:

- checkpoint completeness and digest;
- optimizer and scheduler state;
- RNG and sampler state;
- data cursor and replay/skip behavior;
- model and optimizer shard ownership;
- process-group membership;
- loss, gradient, and tensor sentinels;
- throughput and resource behavior;
- short-run trajectory comparison;
- rollback;
- and observation-window recurrence.

It returns `QUALITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED` when long-horizon model quality was not measured.

## 03.10 Future pack selection

The next pack is selected from evidence, not aspiration.

Candidate score:

```text
material severity
× recurrence or qualification trigger
× native/bundled gap
× evidence availability
× one-party value
× pack reuse
× customer commitment
÷ security, adapter, and experiment burden
```

Candidates:

- numerical divergence through an `OperatorAlignmentBackend`;
- scale-dependent failure through a `ScaleEmulationBackend`;
- workload-specific device dependence with a provider/hardware partner;
- fail-slow reproduction only when existing tools find the slow component but cannot close it;
- heterogeneous post-training actor state after the core IR proves extensible.

- applicability;

```text
+ native tools leave some incidents unresolved or expensive
+ we can execute customer-local experiments
+ a lower-cost faithful case and recovery assurance change our decisions
= internal value without waiting for another company
```

External provider, framework, or hardware participation can increase value. It cannot be a prerequisite for the first sale.

#### 04.2 Primary initial segment

The strongest initial account is a **workload platform or model organization with an internal AI-infrastructure team serving multiple workloads or research groups**.

Required characteristics:

- repeated distributed PyTorch workloads;
- at least one material incident in recent operating history and credible recurrence or stack-change exposure;
- direct control over job launch, images, checkpoints, and evidence capture;
- a platform/reliability owner with authority to run experiments;
- customer-local or VPC execution permitted;
- native diagnostic tools already in use, allowing a fair baseline;
- enough workload diversity for a second case;
- and a budget owner who values research schedule, compute utilization, or engineering capacity.

Preferred characteristics:

- multi-cloud or multi-provider operation;
- several internal model teams;
- repeated framework/runtime upgrades;
- expensive or long-running workloads;
- limited specialist headcount relative to incident load;
- recurring provider escalations;
- or regulated/private data that makes external support difficult.

#### 04.3 Secondary segment: managed training platform or neocloud

A provider is qualified when it:

- sees incidents across several customers;
- competes on support quality and time to restored service;
- lacks a complete internal replay/reduction system;

Potential value:

## 03.17 Detailed initial-pack and scope discipline

### 05 — Initial Incident Pack and Scope Discipline

#### 05.1 One flagship pack

The first commercial-quality pack is:

> **Pre-collective control-flow and distributed-lifecycle contract failure**

It covers a bounded family in which ranks or actors cease to satisfy a declared collective or lifecycle contract because of an upstream control-flow, data-loader, checkpoint, process, or scheduling event.

##### Supported mechanisms

- one rank omits a collective;
- ranks invoke different collective types or payload contracts;
- a data-dependent branch creates divergent collective order;
- a data-loader or checkpoint path prevents one rank from reaching the collective;
- a process exits or changes membership before the expected operation;
- a process group is created, destroyed, or reused inconsistently;
- a bounded runtime/kernel lifecycle event prevents progress and can be distinguished from an ordinary delayed rank.

##### Not included

- generic network diagnosis;
- arbitrary NCCL performance tuning;
- broad fail-slow analysis;
- every deadlock;
- universal kernel hang analysis;
- hardware certification;
- arbitrary pipeline/expert-parallel topologies;
- model-quality failures without a declared distributed contract.

#### 05.2 Why this pack is still viable despite Flight Recorder

Flight Recorder is a mandatory baseline and evidence source. TrainCapsule receives no commercial credit for duplicating:

- collective history;
- missing-rank detection;
- mismatched collective type/shape reporting;
- or source-line identification already available natively.

The pack succeeds only when it performs additional work:

1. reconstruct the upstream trigger or provide a bounded experiment plan;
2. preserve the relevant data/control-flow relation locally;
3. reduce actors, steps, model scope, and data to a cheaper faithful experiment;
4. distinguish omission, delay, process failure, and infrastructure failure;
5. evaluate the proposed guard/recovery state;
6. install an incident contract with a valid applicability envelope;
7. generate a native support/upstream artifact.

#### 05.3 First pack evidence requirements

Required where applicable:

- exact c10d/Flight Recorder version and configuration;
- per-rank collective sequence and lifecycle;
- process-group identity and membership;
- CPU stack and semantic training marker before the boundary;
- global step/microbatch/phase;
- process exit/signal state;
- scheduler placement and lifecycle;
- data-shard/sampler reference when the branch is data-dependent;
- checkpoint operation state when relevant;
- recorder completeness and perturbation profile;
- clean control.

Optional but potentially required by the mechanism:

- selected tensor signature;
- data-loader queue state;
- kernel interval;
- NCCL RAS or network evidence;
- source and substitute node execution.

#### 05.4 First pack incident predicate

```yaml
incidentPack: PRE_COLLECTIVE_LIFECYCLE_CONTRACT_V1
predicate:
  expected:
    allRequiredActorsReachContractBoundary: true
    collectiveTypeAgreement: true
    collectivePayloadAgreement: true
    processGroupMembershipAgreement: true
  observedFailure:
    any:
      - actorOmitsBoundary
      - actorReordersBoundary
      - payloadContractDiffers
      - actorCannotProgressToBoundary
      - membershipChangesIllegally
faithfulness:
  preserve:
    - triggeringControlOrDataRelation
    - firstObservedBoundaryClass
    - requiredActorGroupStructure
  strongestClaim:
    - OBSERVED_BOUNDARY_ONLY
    - MECHANISM_SUPPORTED
    - MECHANISM_REPRODUCED
```

#### 05.5 Native baseline protocol

For every external case, execute:

```text
A. Customer's ordinary logs and process
B. Current stable Flight Recorder / native trace analyzer
C. Native provider or accelerator diagnostic workflow
D. TrainCapsule using exactly the same available evidence
E. TrainCapsule with any additional pre-approved capture
```

Record:

- setup time;
- evidence collection time;
- time to first useful boundary;
- time to discriminating experiment;
- time to faithful reproducer;
- operator hours;
- GPU cost;
- external handoffs;
- recovery assurance;
- and recurrence handling.

TrainCapsule must be evaluated against `C`, not merely against raw logs.

#### 05.6 First pack release gate

A commercial-quality pack requires:

- controlled fault suite including clean controls;
- malformed and partial evidence behavior;
- correct observed boundary on hidden cases;
- no direct observed-boundary-to-blame mapping;
- at least one faithful reduction beyond native rank/source-line output;
- customer-local replay;
- recovery-assurance result;
- incident contract creation;
- security and perturbation tests;
- external operator execution;
- and a documented case where the outcome exceeds native tools.

#### 05.7 Second pack selection

Do not pre-commit to a broad order. Select the next pack using qualified incident evidence.

Candidate A — **Checkpoint/resume state inconsistency**

- strong alignment with Recovery Assurance;
- recurring after node/process recovery and stack changes;
- requires checkpoint, optimizer, RNG, sampler, and data-cursor contracts;
- may create direct internal value without provider acceptance.

Candidate B — **First numerical divergence**

- high value but harder replay, privacy, and instrumentation;
- requires tensor-signature policy, precision identity, and tolerance controls;
- can be amplified by iterative training or post-training.

Candidate C — **Workload-specific suspected hardware corruption**

- potentially high strategic value;
- must be partner-led with matched substitute hardware and strict liability controls;
- not suitable as a broad self-serve pack initially.

Candidate D — **Fail-slow mechanism reproduction**

- crowded by native/platform observability and production systems;
- pursue only when a buyer provides a slowdown that current tools identify but cannot reproduce or close.

Selection evidence:

- number of qualified incidents;
- materiality;
- native-tool gap;
- evidence availability;
- repeatability;
- security feasibility;
- pack reuse potential;
- and buyer commitment.

#### 05.8 Unsupported surface policy

When an incident falls outside the released pack:

```text
UNSUPPORTED_KNOWN_FAMILY
NEW_FAMILY_RESEARCH_CANDIDATE
INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
SECURITY_OR_RIGHTS_BLOCKED
OUT_OF_SCOPE_SERVICE_REQUEST
```

Do not silently extend the pack through bespoke code and then count the case as mature software.

# 04 — Technical Architecture

- **Document date:** 9 August 2026
- **Status:** **Final source of truth**
- **Architecture style:** local-first, import-first, federated-by-default for sensitive execution
- **Stable company boundary:** identity, evidence integrity, experiments, faithfulness, recovery assurance, incident contracts, qualification, and policy
- **Replaceable primitives:** capture, trace analysis, operator alignment, scale emulation, health diagnostics, and provider execution
- **Product relationship:** Close produces bounded evidence and contracts; Qualify reruns them against change; Exchange transports only policy-approved results.

## 04.1 Architectural decision

TrainCapsule is a **customer-local failure reproduction and change-qualification system**.

The first product runs inside the workload owner's or provider's trust boundary. Raw source, data, checkpoints, tensors, topology, and detailed incident evidence remain local unless explicit policy permits export.

The first product is not:

- a hosted multi-tenant observability service;
- an always-on cloud telemetry collector;
- a new GPU scheduler;
- a managed GPU fleet;
- or an autonomous production-control plane.

## 04.3 Four-layer productization model

### Layer A — stable trust core

The trust core owns:

- canonical serialization and content identity;
- workload, environment, change, evidence, experiment, and contract schemas;
- capture completeness and evidence provenance;
- incident IR;
- hypotheses and experiment ledger;
- faithfulness and applicability validation;
- result-state semantics;
- Recovery Assurance;
- contract drift, expiry, and revocation;
- policy, audit, and value records.

Ordinary upstream version changes may not require this layer to change.

### Layer B — replaceable adapters and backends

Adapters and backends consume or execute external systems:

- PyTorch Flight Recorder/c10d traces;
- NCCL RAS/Inspector and provider communication telemetry;
- DCGM/NVML/XID/ECC/device evidence;
- Slurm first, Kubernetes later;
- checkpoint implementations;
- OpGuard-compatible operator alignment;
- PrismLLM-compatible scale emulation;
- provider-specific execution or support APIs;
- hardware/fleet health systems.

Each declares exact versions, fields, assumptions, losses, security boundaries, and permitted claims.

### Layer C — declarative incident and qualification packs

A pack defines:

- symptom and economic qualification;
- required/optional evidence;
- incident predicate;
- invariants and mechanism classes;
- experiment templates;
- legal reductions;
- faithfulness contract;
- recovery properties;
- applicability and expiry;
- native exports;
- hidden controlled faults.

Released packs cannot contain customer-specific executable code in the trust path.

### Layer D — customer policy and private references

Customer-controlled configuration includes:

- workload manifest;
- local source/data/checkpoint references;
- evidence access;
- privacy and retention;
- experiment budgets;
- qualification triggers;
- recovery properties;
- permitted actions and exports;
- legal/data-rights terms.

## 04.4 Core backend interfaces

```python
class EvidenceBackend(Protocol):

    def ingest(self, request: IngestRequest) -> EvidenceBundle: ...

class EmulationBackend(Protocol):
```

## 04.10 Minimum Faithful Experiment compiler

The compiler searches across:

- actors/ranks and process groups;
- node/topology class;
- step/event window;
- microbatches and data records;
- checkpoint delta;
- model layers/experts/operator subgraph;
- tensor shapes and relevant values;
- versions and configuration;
- hardware placement;
- concurrency/background load;
- external services.

Optimization is multi-objective:

```text
GPU count
+ wall-clock duration
+ setup complexity
+ private-data volume
+ hardware specificity
+ operator effort
+ receiver access burden
```

Every candidate has:

- transformation;
- preconditions;
- must-preserve set;
- validator identity;
- experiment results;
- reject reason or accepted status.

Global minimality is never claimed unless the bounded search space proves it.

- immutable inputs;
- exact runner and privacy zone;
- GPU/CPU/memory/storage/network budget;
- retry class;
- infrastructure versus semantic status;
- cancellation and timeout;
- permitted conclusion;
- output digests.

Task classes:


## 04.12 Recovery and qualification

Recovery Assurance and Qualify share one execution engine but different policies.

Recovery evaluates:

- state integrity;
- continuity;
- numerical sentinels;
- throughput/resource behavior;
- canary;
- rollback;
- observation window.

Qualify evaluates:

- whether the old incident recurs;
- whether a different boundary fails;
- whether environment drift invalidates the contract;
- whether private references remain available;
- whether the result remains within the applicability envelope.

A passing reduced test alone cannot authorize production release when the mechanism requires topology- or scale-preserving evidence.

## 04.13 Security architecture

Minimum controls:

- non-root sandbox;
- no arbitrary host mounts;
- network disabled by default;
- resource and expansion limits;
- trusted launch templates;
- customer code treated as untrusted;
- archive traversal/symlink/device-file rejection;
- dependency, license, secret, and vulnerability scans;
- pinned dependencies and OCI digests;
- SBOM and signed provenance;
- no AI model access to private evidence without explicit policy;
- recovery actions disabled by default;
- complete audit.

## 04.16 Performance and reliability budgets

Initial internal gates, not industry facts:

| Operation | Target |
|---|---:|
| Import-only production overhead | effectively zero beyond native sources |
| Optional healthy capture mean overhead | ≤1% target |
| Optional healthy capture hard stop | >2% on supported path |
| Required evidence capture in controlled cases | ≥99% |
| Trigger-to-minimum-freeze | <30 seconds where infrastructure survives |
| 64-actor graph construction | <5 minutes |
| Small deterministic structural experiment | <5 minutes where workload permits |
| Cancellation of local worker | <10 seconds |
| False confirmed hardware attribution | zero |
| Public deterministic example | all declared repetitions pass |
| Qualification state consistency | identical across CLI/API/viewer/artifacts |

Mean overhead alone is insufficient. Tail latency, collective waits, incident rate, memory, CPU, storage, network, and output sentinels must be compared.

## 04.17 Architecture success test

The architecture succeeds when an independent clean or authorized local environment can:

1. verify workload, change, backend, pack, and product identities;
2. inspect exactly what evidence is present and missing;
3. reproduce or execute the declared experiment tier;
4. validate the faithfulness and applicability records;
5. inspect the observed boundary, alternatives, and experiment ledger;
6. evaluate the named recovery properties;
7. create and rerun an expiring incident contract;
8. run a qualification against a changed stack;
9. export a bounded native/federated artifact;
10. explain every `UNKNOWN`, omission, drift, and unsupported conclusion.

Anything less is a report generator or test harness, not the intended product.

#### 03.5 Mechanism and experiment planner

The planner maintains a hypothesis ledger and chooses the next experiment by expected information gain, cost, safety, and disclosure risk.

```python
experiment_utility = (
    expected_hypothesis_separation
    * decision_value
    * execution_feasibility
) / (
    gpu_cost
    + engineer_time
    + privacy_risk
    + perturbation_risk
    + elapsed_time
)
```

This formula is a planning heuristic, not a claim that uncertainty or privacy can be measured perfectly.

Every experiment declares:

- target hypotheses;
- held-constant variables;
- changed variable;
- incident predicate;
- expected outcomes;
- failure and infrastructure states;
- statistical policy;
- resource budget;
- data policy;
- and what conclusion is permitted.

#### 03.6 Minimum Faithful Experiment Compiler

The compiler searches across:

- actor/rank set;
- node and topology class;
- step or event window;
- microbatches and data records;
- checkpoint delta;
- model layers, experts, and operator subgraph;
- tensor shapes and values;
- process groups and communication payloads;
- runtime/driver/framework versions;
- hardware placement;
- concurrency and background load;
- and external service dependencies.

A candidate is accepted only when it preserves the declared faithfulness contract.

```yaml
faithfulness:
  incidentPredicate: COLLECTIVE_CONTRACT_VIOLATION
  observedBoundaryClass: PRE_COLLECTIVE_CONTROL_FLOW_DIVERGENCE
  requiredCausalPreconditions:
    - data-dependent-branch-trigger
    - process-group-membership
  requiredTopologyProperties:
    - cross-node: false
  requiredReplayTier: STRUCTURAL
  allowedNumericalVariation: declared
  privacyPolicy: CUSTOMER_LOCAL_DATA_REFERENCE
  applicabilityEnvelope: sha256:...
```

##### Optimization objective

The compiler minimizes a weighted vector, not one scalar:

```text
GPU count
wall-clock duration
setup complexity
private-data volume
hardware specificity
receiver access burden
operator effort
```

The output records the best candidate found, all rejected candidates, the search budget, and why search stopped.

## 04.19 Observational validity and capture safety

### 07 — Observational Validity and Capture Safety

#### 07.1 Import-first law

The first product should work from existing artifacts whenever possible. Custom instrumentation is introduced only when:

- native evidence cannot support the pack;
- the missing field has a defined decision purpose;
- overhead and perturbation are measured;
- and the customer explicitly approves it.

This improves adoption and reduces the chance that TrainCapsule creates or hides the incident.

#### 07.2 Capture levels

##### C0 — Post-incident native import

- no TrainCapsule runtime agent;
- Flight Recorder, NCCL, scheduler, device, and support artifacts only;
- lowest integration burden;
- limited replay/state reconstruction.

##### C1 — Semantic markers and identity

- workload lock;
- step/microbatch/phase markers;
- checkpoint/RNG/sampler references;
- bounded metadata only.

##### C2 — Selected signatures and local ring buffers

- selected tensor or state signatures;
- application control markers;
- bounded per-actor buffers;
- trigger-based freeze.

##### C3 — Incident-triggered deep capture

- selected kernel/compiler/data-loader/network/storage evidence;
- activated only under a bounded window or reproduction lane.

Do not arm C3 continuously without measured need.

#### 07.3 Perturbation validation

For each capture level, compare capture-on and capture-off using representative workloads.

Measure:

- median, p95, and p99 step time;
- throughput;
- CPU utilization and scheduling;
- GPU utilization and kernel timing;
- memory and pinned-memory usage;
- storage and network rate;
- collective wait distribution;
- timeout and error frequency;
- evidence loss;
- recorder failures;
- and workload output sentinels.

##### Decision states

```text
PERTURBATION_WITHIN_POLICY
PERTURBATION_MATERIAL_BUT_ACCEPTED
PERTURBATION_CHANGES_INCIDENT
PERTURBATION_UNDERPOWERED
UNSAFE
UNKNOWN
```

A low average overhead does not establish observational validity for a timing-sensitive failure.

#### 07.4 Freeze architecture

The freeze path must not depend solely on the failed communication group.

Requirements:

- separate controller or shared-store path;
- minimum diagnostic state written first;
- explicit missing actors;
- bounded timeout;
- no indefinite wait;
- immutable artifact writes;
- optional larger artifacts only after minimum freeze;
- and recorder-health status.

#### 07.5 Tensor and data policy

Raw tensors and training records stay local by default.

Configurable signatures may include:

- shape and dtype;
- non-finite count;
- min/max/mean/variance;
- norms;
- deterministic hash where appropriate;
- quantized or distribution sketches;
- selected masked samples.

Each signature policy declares collision, privacy, sensitivity, and nondeterminism limitations.

#### 07.6 Recorder failure policy

Per workload, choose:

```text
FAIL_OPEN_AND_ALERT
DEGRADE_TO_LOWER_CAPTURE
FAIL_JOB_FOR_HIGH_ASSURANCE
DISABLE_CAPTURE_AND_PRESERVE_EXISTING
```

Recorder failure may not silently alter training state or be hidden in the final report.

## 05.4 Authority hierarchy

| Rank | Authority | Strongest permitted conclusion | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | repeated controlled mechanism experiment | changed variable is causal within tested envelope | not universal |
| A2 | source/substitute or version A/B with strong controls | component/environment dependence supported | residual confounders |
| A3 | exact distributed/runtime/state invariant | named contract was violated | deeper cause may precede boundary |
| A4 | independent provider/vendor/component evidence | corroboration | generic tests may miss workload-specific faults |
| A5 | statistical cross-rank/temporal evidence | anomaly or association | correlation |
| A6 | prior incident contract match | investigation prior | signatures can collide |
| A7 | rule or LLM proposal | candidate experiment only | no final authority |

No majority vote among correlated tools creates truth.

## 05.5 Identity

A high-trust result requires exact identity for every material item:

- source commit and source-tree digest;
- dirty state;
- container digest and SBOM;
- package/compiler/framework build;
- CUDA, NCCL, driver, firmware;
- accelerator identities and topology;
- scheduler, launcher, environment variables;
- model and parallelism configuration;
- checkpoint, optimizer, RNG, sampler, data reference;
- pack, backend, comparator, recorder, reducer, runner, policy, and product versions;
- recovery change and qualification change.

Mutable tags, unknown builds, unpinned policies, or unavailable private references lower the maximum attainable tier.

## 05.9 Replay and evidence tiers

### T0 — evidence-only

The original evidence verifies a bounded inconsistency, but safe or faithful replay is unavailable.

### T1 — structural mechanism experiment

The same distributed, lifecycle, or state-transition contract fails, while exact numerical outputs need not match.

### T2 — deterministic relevant-state experiment

The declared relevant state or output matches at the named boundary under fixed identity and controlled nondeterminism.

### T3 — statistical experiment

The anomaly recurs relative to a predeclared baseline, effect size, trial plan, and threshold.

### T4 — source/substitute dependence experiment

A workload and declared controls are held constant while source/substitute environments differ.

### T5 — topology/scale-preserving emulation

A declared execution graph, communication relation, memory behavior, or selected ranks are reproduced through a validated scale-emulation backend. This tier does not automatically establish semantic correctness outside the measured properties.

The tiers are not a universal strength ladder. The permitted claim depends on the incident predicate and applicability envelope.

## 05.10 Deterministic and statistical policy

For deterministic examples:

- every declared repetition must pass;
- all failed and infrastructure-error trials remain in history;
- exact environment identity is fixed;
- no retry-until-green;
- no post hoc seed selection.

A 20/20 result is an internal release discipline for selected deterministic examples, not proof of zero future failure probability.

For statistical cases, predeclare:

- null and alternative;
- operationally meaningful effect;
- Type I/II error targets;
- baseline;
- trial count/stopping rule;
- confounders;
- missing-data treatment.

When zero failures occur in `n` independent trials, the approximate one-sided 95% rule-of-three upper bound is `3/n`; zero observed failures do not establish zero probability.

## 05.11 Minimum Faithful Experiment

TrainCapsule does not promise the globally smallest reproducer.

A `MinimumFaithfulExperiment` is:

> the lowest-cost candidate found within a declared search space, resource budget, wall-clock budget, privacy policy, and faithfulness predicate.

A candidate must preserve:

1. triggering preconditions;
2. incident pack;
3. violated invariant or mechanism;
4. observed-boundary class;
5. required topology/state relation;
6. replay/evidence tier;
7. relevant component dependence or ambiguity;
8. applicability and legal/export validity.

Every reduction trial records:

```yaml
trial:
  id: ...
  parent: sha256:...
  transformation: ...
  preconditions: []
  mustPreserve: []
  validator: sha256:...
  environment: sha256:...
  outcome: ACCEPTED | REJECTED | INFRASTRUCTURE_ERROR
  rejectReason: ...

```

Search stops with a reason:

- source;
- model/checkpoint;

- private topology;

- product code;

- declarative packs;

- approved anonymous signatures/outcomes.

Permission to investigate one case does not imply permission to train a model, publish, retain raw evidence, correlate customers, or create a benchmark.

## 05.22 Dispute, correction, and revocation

Any credible contradictory evidence changes the state to `DISPUTED`.

A result is revoked when:

- identity or integrity fails;
- privacy/export policy was violated;
- faithfulness was false;
- a stronger controlled result contradicts it;
- hardware/component attribution was wrong;
- the contract expired or dependencies disappeared;
- or the customer/legal owner revokes permitted use.

Revocation propagates to derived qualifications and public claims.

- timestamp skew;
- missing rank;

## 05.24 Release gates

- every result names evidence/replay tier;
- every disposition names authority and alternatives;
- `UNKNOWN` survives all surfaces;
- no causal claim from observed boundary alone;
- no hardware confirmation from telemetry alone;
- every accepted reduction preserves declared faithfulness;
- statistical plans are predeclared;
- sanitization is followed by replay;
- federated results verify signer, identity, and scope;
- capsule execution passes security tests;
- stale/expired contracts cannot pass qualification;
- independent operator can execute supported workflow;
- false public attribution blocks release;
- value claims retain attempted-case denominator.

## 05.26 Detailed truth, causality, replay, and statistics

### 06 — Truth, Causality, Replay, and Statistical Policy

#### 06.1 Governing truth chain

```text
raw source evidence
        ↓
verified execution identity
        ↓
observational validity and capture completeness
        ↓
first observed inconsistent boundary
        ↓
competing mechanism hypotheses
        ↓
predeclared discriminating experiments
        ↓
mechanism support or bounded uncertainty
        ↓
recovery-assurance properties
        ↓
incident contract and applicability envelope
```

The product must not begin with a root-cause narrative and search for confirming evidence.

#### 06.2 Authority hierarchy

| Rank | Authority | Permitted conclusion | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | repeated controlled mechanism experiment | declared variable is causal within the tested envelope | may not generalize beyond envelope |
| A2 | source/substitute or version A/B with strong controls | environment/component dependence supported | unobserved differences may remain |
| A3 | exact distributed/runtime invariant | contract violation at named boundary | deeper initiating cause may precede it |
| A4 | independent component health or provider evidence | corroborating component evidence | generic tests can miss workload-specific faults |
| A5 | statistical cross-rank or temporal evidence | anomaly or association | correlation is not causation |
| A6 | prior incident contract match | investigation prior and experiment recommendation | similar signatures can differ |
| A7 | rule or LLM hypothesis | candidate only | never final authority |

A support-ticket closure, human opinion, or model consensus does not outrank contradictory controlled evidence.

#### 06.3 Capture completeness states

```text
COMPLETE_FOR_DECLARED_PACK
COMPLETE_WITH_OPTIONAL_GAPS
PARTIAL_REQUIRED_EVIDENCE
CORRUPTED
UNTRUSTED_IDENTITY
PERTURBATION_UNVALIDATED
UNAVAILABLE
```

A partial capture may support a narrow observed boundary. It cannot be described as complete.

#### 06.4 Replay and evidence tiers

##### T0 — Evidence-only boundary

- original evidence verifies;
- a bounded inconsistent boundary is present;
- replay is unsafe, unavailable, or impractical;
- no mechanism claim beyond evidence.

##### T1 — Structural mechanism replay

- the same distributed/lifecycle contract fails;
- numerical outputs need not match;
- required topology and control preconditions are preserved.

##### T2 — Deterministic relevant-state replay

- the named relevant state or output matches at the declared boundary;
- exact input and environment constraints verify;
- nondeterministic inputs are absent or controlled.

##### T3 — Statistical replay

- the anomaly recurs against a predeclared baseline and threshold;
- trial count and power are justified;
- uncertainty and failed trials are retained.

##### T4 — Source/substitute dependence experiment

- workload and declared controls are held constant;
- source and substitute environments produce materially different outcomes;
- residual confounders are named.

The tiers are not a simple strength ladder for every case. A T4 hardware dependence experiment may still rely on T1 structural evidence.

#### 06.5 Deterministic repeat policy

“20/20” may remain an internal release gate for selected deterministic public examples, but it is not a universal statistical proof.

For a claimed deterministic case:

- all repetitions must pass;
- environment identity must remain fixed;
- trial order and seeds are retained;
- no failed trial may be deleted;
- infrastructure retries are separate;
- and the product must state that the sample does not establish behavior on untested environments.

#### 06.6 Statistical cases

Before execution, define:

- null and alternative hypotheses;
- outcome metric;
- minimum effect of operational interest;
- acceptable Type I and Type II error;
- baseline distribution;
- trial budget and stopping rule;
- confounders;
- and missing-data handling.

Use confidence intervals or a justified nonparametric method. Do not select a threshold after viewing the favorable result.

##### Zero-failure interpretation

When zero recurrences occur in `n` independent trials, the approximate one-sided 95% upper bound on the true failure probability is `3/n` under the common rule-of-three approximation. Therefore:

- 0/20 does not establish a failure probability of zero;
- 0/100 still leaves an approximate upper bound near 3%;
- and independence assumptions must be justified.

Use this to constrain recovery and incident-contract language.

## 05.27 Detailed security, privacy, licensing, and federated verification

### 08 — Security, Privacy, Licensing, and Federated Verification

#### 08.1 Default security posture

> **Customer-local evidence, customer-controlled storage, minimum necessary capture, signed experiment plans, explicit export, and no mandatory external telemetry.**

TrainCapsule handles unusually sensitive material:

- source and generated code;
- model architecture and configuration;
- checkpoints and optimizer state;
- training examples and data references;
- tensor signatures;
- infrastructure topology;
- component vulnerabilities;
- device health;
- and incident history.

Security failure can outweigh every technical benefit.

#### 08.2 Execution and export tiers

##### E0 — Local evidence analysis

- raw evidence never leaves;
- only local reports;
- no portable code.

##### E1 — Federated experiment

- signed experiment plan enters the environment;
- approved local references are resolved there;
- signed result and bounded evidence exit.

##### E2 — Private portable reproducer

- transferred only to a named authorized receiver;
- explicit license and data authorization;
- sandbox and identity lock required.

##### E3 — Sanitized portable reproducer

- sanitization preserves predicate and is replayed;
- no restricted material;
- receiver can execute independently.

##### E4 — Public capsule

- public code/data or legally cleared transformations;
- clean-environment execution;
- no private credentials or references.

The product must succeed commercially at E0/E1 for sensitive customers.

#### 08.3 Federated experiment protocol

```text
planner creates immutable experiment request
        ↓
customer policy engine verifies request and signer
        ↓
local runner resolves approved private references
        ↓
sandbox executes experiment
        ↓
runner records environment and result digests
        ↓
attestation and bounded evidence are signed
        ↓
external verifier checks identity, policy, and result
```

A receiving organization may trust the attestation, request its own run, or dispute the methodology. TrainCapsule does not force trust.

#### 08.4 Confidential computing

Where supported, remote attestation can cryptographically prove CPU/GPU guest state before secrets or encrypted artifacts are released. NVIDIA documents confidential-container architectures with CPU/GPU attestation and policy-controlled secret release. [S21][S22]

Constraints must be explicit:

- supported GPU and CPU platforms;
- multi-GPU topology limitations;
- performance overhead;
- operator and Kubernetes requirements;
- attestation-service dependencies;
- and unsupported networks/storage paths.

Confidential computing is an optional deployment mode, not the universal answer to cross-company trust.

#### 08.5 Sandbox requirements

- rootless or non-root execution;
- read-only root filesystem;
- dropped capabilities;
- no host Docker socket;
- no SSH agent or host home;
- no cloud metadata;
- network disabled by default;
- dedicated input/output mounts;
- CPU, memory, PID, disk, GPU, and time limits;
- seccomp/AppArmor or equivalent where available;
- customer code treated as untrusted;
- complete audit;
- and explicit cleanup.

#### 08.6 Archive and artifact safety

Reject:

- path traversal;
- absolute paths;
- escaping symlinks or hardlinks;
- device files and FIFOs;
- ownership manipulation;
- decompression bombs;
- duplicate conflicting paths;
- unexpected executable bits;
- oversized expansion;
- malformed nested structures;
- and unverifiable digests.

#### 08.7 Supply-chain identity

Use existing standards:

- OCI images and artifacts;
- OCI subject/referrer relationships;
- Sigstore/Cosign or approved private PKI;
- in-toto attestations;
- SBOMs;
- pinned dependencies and immutable images;
- isolated builds;
- signed releases;
- vulnerability and license scanning.

OCI v1.1 supports artifact types, subjects, and referrers; Cosign supports artifact signatures and policy-checked in-toto attestations. [S23][S24]

TrainCapsule should define incident semantics, not invent a new cryptographic transport.

Every artifact has:

Customer owns or controls:

- aggregate metrics;
- or permissioned incident signatures.

One investigation does not grant rights to train a model, publish the case, retain raw evidence, or correlate customers.

#### 08.10 Licensing and redistribution

A faithful reproducer may contain code or artifacts whose licenses prohibit redistribution or derivative publication.

Every export must record:

- component licenses;
- customer and third-party rights;
- model/data redistribution restrictions;
- generated-code provenance;
- patch or test licensing;
- and receiver authorization.

When rights are insufficient, use a local reference or federated experiment. Do not copy private code into a supposedly sanitized public case.

#### 08.11 Retention, expiry, and revocation

- raw evidence: shortest customer-approved period;
- temporary replay data: delete after verified run;
- private incident contract: contract and policy duration;
- public artifact: indefinite only with correction/revocation mechanism;
- signature/outcome metadata: explicit permission duration;
- secrets: never stored.

Revocation propagates to derived artifacts where contract or law requires it.

#### 08.12 Recovery-action safety

Initial product actions are advisory or isolated.

Prohibited by default:

- draining production nodes;
- restarting clusters;
- changing timeouts;
- modifying model code;
- replacing hardware;
- deleting checkpoints;
- changing network/storage configuration.

Future production actions require:

- explicit policy;
- least privilege;
- human or customer-approved authorization;
- canary;
- rollback;
- blast-radius limit;
- audit;
- and independent verification.

# 09 — Productization and Service Boundary

## 09.1 Productization law

> **A case is software-led only when released engine, adapter, and incident-pack components produce the trust-critical result without customer-specific trust-core code or undocumented founder judgment.**

This does not require zero human involvement. High-value infrastructure products may include expert support. The distinction is whether expert work operates a stable system or recreates the system for each customer.

## 09.2 Four-layer architecture

### Layer A — Stable trust core

- canonical identity and digests;
- evidence and provenance store;
- incident IR;
- capture-completeness and perturbation states;
- hypothesis and experiment ledger;
- reduction legality and faithfulness checks;
- replay verdicts;
- disposition status model;
- recovery-assurance framework;
- incident-contract lifecycle;
- privacy, export, revocation, and audit.

Trust-core changes require normative specification, adversarial review, migration, and release audit.

### Layer B — Versioned evidence and execution adapters

- Flight Recorder/c10d;
- NCCL evidence;
- scheduler and process lifecycle;
- device and node evidence;
- checkpoint system;
- container/runtime;
- provider support format;
- future actor/orchestrator systems.

Adapters declare semantic loss and cannot modify truth policy.

### Layer C — Declarative incident packs

A pack contains:

- supported mechanism family;
- required evidence;
- observed invariants;
- hypothesis templates;
- allowed experiments;
- faithfulness predicates;
- reduction operators;
- statistical policy;
- recovery property set;
- applicability envelope;
- clean controls;
- fault corpus;
- and export mappings.

Executable pack extensions are reviewed and sandboxed. Configuration text alone cannot become arbitrary code.

### Layer D — Customer policy and references

- workload manifests;
- private artifact references;
- capture level;
- retention and export policy;
- experiment budget;
- approved recovery actions;
- support endpoints;
- incident severity and SLO.

Customer configuration cannot weaken released truth or security rules.

## 09.3 Productization metrics

For every case, record separately:

### Code and architecture

- trust-core files changed;
- released adapter or pack changed;
- customer-specific executable code;
- schema migration;
- mock or unsupported substitution;
- new external dependency.

### Operation

- installation and integration hours;
- customer operator hours;
- TrainCapsule expert hours;
- founder-only interpretation hours;
- time to first useful evidence;
- time to faithful experiment;
- GPU and cloud cost;
- failed experiment cost;
- security/legal review time.

### Economics

- contract revenue;
- direct engineering labor;
- direct compute/storage;
- customer-specific support;
- security/compliance cost;
- gross margin;
- renewal/second-case result.

## 09.4 Evidence-based productization gates

The following are management gates, not external market facts.

### Second case in the same released pack

- no trust-core rewrite;
- no customer-specific code in the trust path;
- operator runbook completes;
- founder-only analysis is lower than the first case;
- direct delivery cost is recorded;
- and the customer can explain the result from product artifacts.

### Third case in the same pack

- most changes are adapter compatibility, configuration, or pack data;
- installation and time-to-first-evidence trend downward;
- direct margin improves or remains intentionally service-grade;
- no hidden manual data transformation;
- and a non-founder operator can execute the workflow.

### Mature supported pack

- bounded setup;
- predictable evidence requirements;
- released conformance suite;
- known unsupported conditions;
- customer-specific code is exceptional and separately sold;
- direct margin supports the chosen business model.

Do not use one percentage to substitute for these facts.

## 09.5 Operator-independence test

A trained external operator must be able to:

1. register and lock a workload;
2. run readiness analysis;
3. import or freeze evidence;
4. inspect capture gaps and perturbation status;
5. execute the pack;
6. review observed divergence and hypotheses;
7. approve bounded experiments;
8. run the federated/local runner;
9. inspect disposition and recovery assurance;
10. create or update an incident contract;
11. export a provider/upstream package;
12. process revocation or expiry.

Founder support may exist. Hidden founder edits invalidate self-serve claims.

## 09.6 Productized-service path

A software-assisted expert service is a valid outcome when:

- incidents are high value;
- customers pay repeatedly;
- expertise is scarce;
- engagements are bounded;
- tooling reduces cost and improves quality;
- customer-specific work creates reusable adapters or packs where rights permit;
- and marketing accurately describes the service component.

A service path is not a failure if it produces strong margin and meets the founder’s income goal. It is a failure only when it is mislabeled as autonomous software or cannot scale to the intended economics.

## 09.7 Service-trap indicators

- every incident needs new capture architecture;
- no stable workload or evidence contract;
- manual log reading dominates;
- reduction depends on undocumented expert intuition;
- customer-specific code enters the trust core;
- no repeat use;
- unbounded emergency support;
- low or falling gross margin;
- no operator independence;
- no durable incident contract;
- or revenue grows only with senior headcount.

## 09.8 New-family research path

Unknown incident families are handled through a separate research workflow:

```text
case qualification
→ evidence feasibility
→ manual research under explicit service scope
→ mechanism and pack candidate
→ controlled fault corpus
→ hidden adversarial tests
→ release decision
```

Research cases are not counted as mature pack success.

# 10 — Defensibility Without Assuming a Standard or Data Moat

## 10.1 Base defensibility

The company must be defendable even when:

- every customer keeps raw data local;
- no cross-customer signature is retained;
- providers use their own support formats;
- the open protocol has modest adoption;
- and native tools continue improving.

The base defensibility stack is:

1. **Deep workflow integration** into workload identity, incident operations, recovery checks, and stack-change qualification.
2. **Customer-local incident history** and applicability-aware contracts.
3. **Pack quality** proven on difficult, supported mechanism families.
4. **Federated execution and security architecture** that fits sensitive environments.
5. **Trust history** including explicit unknowns, disputes, revocations, and no unsupported blame.
6. **Operator experience** and a specialized systems team.
7. **Native-tool leverage** that improves as first-party evidence improves rather than competing with it.
8. **Deployment and support reliability** across customer environments.

## 10.2 Optional defensibility multipliers

### Open protocol adoption

Valuable only when independent organizations create, verify, or exchange artifacts.

### Permissioned outcome knowledge

Valuable only when contracts permit retention and confirmed outcomes improve later experiment selection.

### Provider integration

Valuable when embedded in normal ticket, field-engineering, hardware-health, or node-lifecycle operations.

### Confidential/attested runner network

Valuable when customers repeatedly need cross-boundary verification and cannot share source artifacts.

### Ecosystem packs

Valuable when external pack authors and maintainers contribute under conformance rules.

None may be claimed before observed.

## 10.3 Incumbent response test

At every six-month strategic review, ask:

1. Can current Flight Recorder/native tools produce the same experiment?
2. Can the cloud provider close the case through its existing support bundle?
3. Can a customer script the workflow with ordinary CI and containers?
4. Has a provider added workload reduction, recovery assurance, or incident contracts?
5. Does TrainCapsule still reduce cross-layer and private-workload friction?
6. Is the recurring value in operation, or only in one algorithm?

If one incumbent offers the full outcome for the target account at lower switching cost, choose among:

- integrate as a pack/adapter;
- move to a cross-provider or private-workload boundary;
- specialize in a mechanism the incumbent does not close;
- provide expert service on top;
- or stop that product surface.

## 10.4 Open-source boundary

### Open candidates

- workload and environment lock schema;
- incident IR core schema;
- replay/disposition status semantics;
- local verifier;
- public flagship pack subset;
- fault-injection examples;
- adapter SDK;
- provider/upstream export templates;
- OCI media types and signature rules;
- selected reducers and public incident contracts.

### Commercial/private candidates

- supported enterprise adapters;
- federated/VPC orchestration;
- private incident packs;
- security and air-gap distribution;
- protected-workload scheduling and contract operations;
- expert incident response;
- provider workflow integrations;
- customer-specific policy;
- permissioned outcome intelligence;
- SLA and support.

Open source is used to establish technical trust and distribution. It does not create defensibility by itself.

## 10.5 Standard strategy

### Stage A — Internal canonical model

Use the format internally and publish no “standard” claim.

### Stage B — Open verifier and exports

Publish schemas and verification; prioritize conversion into existing provider/framework formats.

### Stage C — Independent implementation

A standardization effort becomes credible only when at least one external tool independently creates or verifies the artifacts.

### Stage D — Multi-organization working group

Proceed only when several independent organizations actively need shared semantics.

### Stage E — Neutral governance

Consider only when neutrality materially increases adoption and the commercial company is already defendable through operations, integrations, packs, and service.

## 10.6 Trust moat

Track and disclose where appropriate:

- `UNKNOWN` rate;
- unsupported-case rate;
- observed-boundary accuracy on controlled cases;
- mechanism-reproduction rate;
- false component-dependence claims;
- disputes;
- revoked artifacts;
- security incidents;
- contract expiry rate;
- reduced-test/full-canary disagreement;
- external operator success;
- and recovery recurrence.

A trust product that reports only wins has no trust moat.

## 10.7 Naming and positioning boundary

Do not call TrainCapsule “vendor-neutral” in initial commercial claims unless it supports and is independently used across more than one relevant provider or accelerator ecosystem.

Permitted initial positioning:

> **Portable, customer-controlled incident evidence for supported PyTorch/NCCL workloads.**

Long-term neutrality is an earned property.

## 10.8 Defensibility-decay and upstream-absorption test

Defensibility is recalculated for every released pack and contract:

```python
remaining_private_operational_value = (
    environment_materialization_value
    + controlled_experiment_value
    + recovery_assurance_value
    + incident_history_value
    + integration_and_response_value
    - native_feature_overlap
    - ordinary_repository_test_value
    - adapter_maintenance_cost
)
```

This is an accounting framework, not a universal valuation formula.

Required decisions:

- when a defect becomes an ordinary test, upstream or hand it off;
- when a provider bundles the full outcome, integrate or exit that surface;
- when private execution and recovery assurance remain valuable, retain the commercial workflow;
- when contracts expire faster than they are reused, change packaging;
- when pack operation requires expert judgment but has strong margin, label it productized service;
- when no durable operational value remains, stop claiming a moat.

No number of stored artifacts substitutes for current switching cost and repeat customer behavior.

# 12 — Go-to-Market and Discovery

## 12.1 Discovery goal

The goal is not to collect positive opinions. It is to determine:

- which incidents remain expensive after current native tooling;
- who controls the required evidence and experiments;
- whether a faithful lower-cost case changes the outcome;
- whether recovery-state assurance is valued;
- whether recurrence or stack-change qualification creates repeat demand;

## 12.2 Discovery stopping rule

Interview enough qualified operators to reach mechanism and buyer saturation rather than relying on a vanity count.

Operating rule:

- begin with at least 15 qualified operators across workload-owner and provider roles;
- collect detailed timelines for at least 8 real incidents;
- continue until five consecutive qualified interviews introduce no new fatal workflow, substitute, or procurement obstacle;
- and obtain at least two organizations willing to provide a bounded real or faithfully reconstructed case.

These counts are management choices, not statistical proof of a market.

## 12.3 Interview protocol

For one real incident, ask:

1. What exact workload and parallelism structure ran?
2. What was the first symptom?
3. Which native tools were enabled?
4. Did automatic recovery occur?
5. What remained unresolved after recovery?
6. What evidence was missing?
7. How long to a useful boundary, experiment, disposition, and resumed run?
8. Which teams or companies were involved?
9. Was the mechanism confirmed or only inferred?
10. Did the incident recur?
11. Was a durable regression produced?
12. What data could not leave the environment?
13. Would a signed local experiment have been acceptable?
14. Who had authority to run a substitute or canary?
15. What did the incident cost in compute and specialist time?
16. Which existing product or internal script was the substitute?
17. Would a lower-cost faithful experiment change the decision?
18. Would recovery-state checks change confidence?
19. What would trigger a second purchase?
20. What resource will the organization commit now?

Do not ask only “Would you use this?”

# 13 — Validation System and Company Gates

## 13.1 Evidence ladder

| Stage | Evidence | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | design and schemas | coherent design only |
| E1 | controlled injected faults | selected mechanism is detectable |
| E2 | public/historical case | pack can reconstruct a bounded known case |
| E3 | current external case | works outside founder environment |
| E4 | native-tool advantage | creates a materially better outcome than best reasonable substitute |
| E5 | operational value | changes time, cost, decision, or recurrence handling |
| E6 | second and third use | may be a repeatable product/service |
| E7 | payment or sponsorship | revealed willingness to pay |
| E8 | renewal/workflow dependence | recurring business and strategic value become credible |

No stage is inferred from a lower one.

## 13.2 Required validation portfolios

### Portfolio A — controlled fault lab

Purpose:

- test truth states;
- benchmark capture perturbation;
- reject false attribution;
- test malformed and missing evidence;
- validate security.

### Portfolio B — public or historical incident

Purpose:

- compare with a known mechanism;
- produce an inspectable case;
- establish public technical authority.

### Portfolio C — current external incident

Purpose:

- test real constraints, value, deployment, operator independence, and commercial action.

### Portfolio D — stack-change/contract revalidation

Purpose:

- test whether recurring incident contracts remain useful rather than turning into stale tests.

## 13.3 Native-tool benchmark gate

For every external case, compare against the latest applicable:

- framework trace analyzer;
- accelerator/runtime diagnostics;
- cloud/provider support process;
- customer internal scripts;
- automatic recovery system.

A product result passes only when it produces at least one material incremental outcome:

- lower-cost faithful experiment;
- mechanism separation native tools did not produce;
- recovery-state assurance;
- durable incident contract;
- materially fewer support rounds;
- or a previously inaccessible provider/upstream artifact.

## 13.4 Technical gates

### Identity and evidence

- all required identities verified;
- missing evidence cannot appear complete;
- malformed input is bounded and rejected;
- raw and normalized evidence linked;
- adapter loss disclosed.

### Perturbation

- capture impact measured on representative workloads;
- no recurrent incident creation or masking in supported mode;
- memory/storage bounded;
- recorder failure explicit.

### Observed boundary

- correct boundary on hidden controlled cases;
- clean controls remain clean;
- alternative explanations preserved;
- no boundary-to-blame shortcut.

### Faithful experiment

- declared predicate and envelope preserved;
- lower cost or access burden measured;
- rejected candidates retained;
- search budget and stopped reason disclosed;
- reduced/full-canary disagreement tested.

### Security

- sandbox and archive attack suite;
- no secret or restricted artifact exported;
- signed materials and provenance verify;
- federated policy denied correctly;
- deletion and revocation tested.

### Recovery assurance

- named state properties checked;
- rollback exists;
- clean control passes;
- performance and numerical sentinels evaluated;
- observation window stated;
- long-horizon quality limitations explicit.

## 13.5 Economic materiality

A case is material when the customer independently agrees that the operational change justifies total product and integration cost.

Evidence may include:

- a multi-day unresolved incident becomes a same-day faithful experiment;
- specialist investigation effort falls substantially;
- compute waste falls materially;
- a blocked program resumes;
- a repeated support dispute is narrowed or closed;
- a dangerous or invalid recovery is rejected;
- a prior incident contract catches a real regression;
- or an annual platform/support workflow saves repeat effort.

No fixed dollar threshold is universal. Absolute value and recurrence must be reported.

## 13.6 Commercial gate

Broad product investment requires at least one:

- paid incident engagement followed by a second paid use;
- annual critical-workload assurance contract;
- provider-sponsored multi-customer program;
- paid provider evidence-gateway integration;
- accepted upstream/provider artifact plus a funded follow-on case.

A free proof of concept with no second-use action does not unlock broad platform scope.

## 13.7 Productization gate

By the third case in one released pack:

- trust core remains stable;
- customer-specific code is absent from the trust path;
- founder-only hours fall;
- deployment and time-to-first-evidence improve;
- direct economics are understood;
- and an external operator runs the case.

Failure routes the business to productized service, research, redesign, or stop.

## 13.8 Case ledger and anti-gaming rules

- register case before result;
- retain rejected and unsupported cases;
- predeclare thresholds;
- include integration and failed experiments in cost;
- separate infrastructure errors;
- retain every trial;
- no automatic golden-output update;
- customer approves economic record;
- no favorable-case-only averages;
- report denominator and confidence.

## 13.10 Hard kill conditions

Stop or narrow when a condition persists after one bounded remediation cycle:

| Condition | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Native tools produce the same outcome | no unique value | upstream/integrate/stop pack |
| Faithful experiment cannot lower cost or access burden | core promise fails | redesign or stop |
| Reduced tests disagree recurrently with higher-fidelity canaries | invalid faithfulness | block pack |
| Capture materially alters incident | observational invalidity | import-only or stop capture |
| Security requires prohibited data export | procurement blocker | federate or disqualify |
| Recovery assurance overclaims quality | trust liability | block release |
| False confirmed hardware/component claim | severe trust failure | revoke, investigate, suspend |
| Third same-pack case needs trust-core rewrite | no stable product | service/research only |
| No second use after material first result | episodic/low priority | do not build recurring platform |
| Customers will not pay or sponsor meaningful resources | weak revealed demand | career/open-source or pivot |

## 13.11 Competitive-bundle gate

Before a case counts as native-tool advantage:

1. enumerate every capability already included in the customer's framework, cloud, support, observability, and orchestration contracts;
2. run or document the best approved native workflow;
3. include internal expert and approved agent time;
4. include TrainCapsule deployment, security, experiment, and support cost;
5. identify the exact incremental outcome;
6. obtain the buyer's written or auditable statement that the incremental outcome is valuable.

A result that beats raw logs but not the bundled production workflow is not commercial proof.

## 13.12 Contract-value-decay gate

For each installed incident contract, measure at every revalidation:

- whether the original mechanism remains relevant;
- whether the test has been upstreamed;
- whether native tools now cover it;
- whether the environment can still be materialized;
- whether the applicability envelope still matches the protected workload;
- whether execution changed a release or recovery decision;
- and direct maintenance cost.

Classify:

```text
ACTIVE_OPERATIONAL_VALUE
UPSTREAMED_NO_PRIVATE_VALUE
STALE_REQUIRES_RESEARCH
EXPIRED_ENVIRONMENT
NATIVE_FEATURE_ABSORBED
CUSTOMER_RETIRED
```

Annual assurance is justified only by current operational value, not by the number of historical contracts.

## 13.13 Experiment-capacity and time-to-decision gate

A case plan must declare:

- maximum GPUs/nodes;
- maximum elapsed time;
- maximum direct compute/storage cost;
- allowed production-capacity interference;
- decision deadline;
- source/substitute environment availability;
- and abort conditions.

If the planner cannot produce useful evidence within the customer's decision window and budget, return `UNSUPPORTED_OR_UNECONOMIC` rather than consuming capacity indefinitely.

## 13.14 Procurement and continuity gate

Before production use, verify:

- the customer can approve the deployment before the incident window closes;
- TrainCapsule is not a required training-path dependency;
- disable/uninstall and evidence export work;
- support scope matches staffed capability;
- security and continuity materials satisfy the qualified buyer;

# 19 — AI Engineering Factory

## 19.1 Governing principle

> **AI may supply implementation labor. It may not define truth, modify protected expected evidence, certify its own work, or invent external facts.**

The engineering factory separates:

```text
research and source verification
→ normative specification
→ implementation
→ adversarial counterexample generation
→ real integration audit
→ perturbation/performance audit
→ security audit
→ value adversary
→ read-only release
→ external operation
```

## 19.2 Roles

| Role | Responsibility | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|
| Research agent | verify current primary-source contracts | infer undocumented behavior as fact |
| Product/spec agent | define bounded outcome, statuses, schemas, gates | implement and approve same task |
| Builder agent | implement allowed scope | edit normative truth or hidden tests |
| Adversarial agent | find false green and concrete counterexamples | approve its own repair |
| Integration auditor | prove real upstream/runtime path executed | accept mocks for required path |
| Perturbation/performance agent | measure distributional impact and cost | optimize without baseline |
| Security agent | attack sandbox, export, supply chain, policy | weaken policy to pass |
| Statistical reviewer | verify pre-registration and analysis | select favorable thresholds post hoc |
| Value adversary | test native advantage and economics | accept technical pass as customer value |
| Release agent | run clean checkout and hidden gates read-only | modify source during certification |
| External evidence recorder | store customer/payment/provider facts | simulate or reinterpret missing facts |
| Orchestrator | schedule work, contexts, Git, checkpoints | override failed hard gate |

Separate sessions of the same model are useful process separation, not full epistemic independence. Machine execution, hidden tests, primary sources, and qualified humans remain necessary.

## 19.3 Protected assets

```text
specs/normative/
schemas/released/
tests/hidden/
tests/clean-controls/
tests/faithfulness/
tests/hardware-disposition/
policies/release/
policies/public-claims/
policies/recovery/
evidence/external/
value/external/
security/baselines/
```

Builder tasks cannot modify protected assets.

Changes require:

- specification task;
- source/evidence rationale;
- ADR;
- adversarial review;
- human review for trust-critical policy;
- read-only release approval.

## 19.5 Work-unit discipline

A work unit:

- produces one verifiable result;
- touches one primary subsystem;
- has explicit authority and failure states;
- fits a fresh context;
- ends in one coherent commit;
- and records the next dependency.

Avoid “build the incident compiler.” Split into schema, pure logic, storage, runner, pack integration, hidden faults, E2E, and documentation.

## 19.6 Context management

Every fresh session receives:

- task packet;
- exact source/spec excerpts;
- public interfaces;
- selected files;
- failing machine output;
- previous handoff;
- no unrelated repository dump.

Start a fresh context when:

- subsystem changes;
- implementation hands to audit;
- several failed approaches pollute context;
- a major architecture decision appears;
- tests become green and certification begins;
- rate limits or context quality degrade.

| Failure mode | Control |
|---|---|

## 19.12 Orchestrator rules

# 08 — Acquisition Thesis

- **Document date:** 9 August 2026
- **Status:** **Strategic design objective; never a customer promise**
- **Governing rule:** TrainCapsule must create independent customer value. Acquisition is a consequence of strategic dependence, not a substitute for product-market fit.

## 08.1 Executive conclusion

A complete TrainCapsule repository is not acquisition-shaped.

Large AI companies can reproduce:

- a recorder;
- event normalization;
- a graph;
- an experiment planner;
- an LLM incident assistant;
- a reduction loop;
- a test runner;
- or a provider ticket generator.

A buyer can also hire the founder or reproduce a narrow feature.

TrainCapsule becomes acquisition-shaped only when ownership protects assets that cannot be obtained as cheaply through hiring, partnership, open source, or internal implementation:

```text
important recurring workflow
+ deployed customer/provider trust
+ qualification and incident continuity
+ private integrations
+ recognized open technical authority
+ measured economic value
+ specialized team
+ competitive-denial or platform-attach value
= plausible ownership case
```

No acquisition is guaranteed.

## 08.2 The correct Brev lesson

Brev is relevant as a company-building pattern, not as a product template.

The useful lesson, based on NVIDIA's current Brev product and acquisition evidence, is: [S34]

1. choose a high-friction accelerated-computing workflow;
2. make the before/after improvement obvious;
3. remove several steps rather than add another dashboard;
4. make the workflow attach naturally to a larger platform;
5. earn adoption and close collaboration;
6. let ownership become strategically rational.

For TrainCapsule, the analogous before/after is:

```text
large private incident
+ scattered native evidence
+ uncertain mechanism
+ expensive reproduction
+ risky restart
+ repeated support handoff

becomes

bounded faithful experiment
+ named evidence limits
+ recovery-state assurance
+ failure-derived qualification contract
+ native/federated support artifact
```

The product must never claim that this analogy proves NVIDIA or another company will acquire it.

## 08.3 What recent acquisitions imply

Official acquisition announcements in adjacent infrastructure markets show recurring patterns. [S31][S32][S33][S34]

- Astral's developer tools already powered millions of workflows and fit directly into the acquirer's coding ecosystem.
- Bun had broad adoption, became important infrastructure for Claude Code, and had months of close partnership before acquisition.
- Neptune provided a specialized experiment/training-analysis workflow directly relevant to frontier-model development.
- Similar platform acquisitions expand a buyer into adjacent developer, model, or operational workflow.

The pattern is not “technically impressive repository.” It is:

```text
adopted or strategically dependent workflow
+ direct product/revenue/research acceleration
+ team and operating knowledge
+ close partnership
= ownership discussion
```

## 08.4 TrainCapsule ownership thesis

A strategic buyer could rationally prefer ownership when TrainCapsule already provides several of:

- lower support and incident-handling cost;
- faster return to productive training or post-training;
- safer stack/hardware upgrades;
- reduced false node quarantine or RMA;
- customer-controlled evidence across cloud boundaries;
- failure-derived qualification used in normal releases;
- private workload contracts that improve the buyer's platform;
- a trusted open verifier/protocol;
- a specialized team spanning training, GPU systems, security, and field engineering;
- and competitive risk if another platform owned the workflow.

### AI-native cloud or managed training platform

Main reason not to acquire:

# Final Directive

Build the complete local Close–Qualify–Exchange system. Make the flagship incident pack technically rigorous, make the checkpoint/resume pack strategically relevant to improving native recovery, and make every advanced diagnostic or scale-emulation primitive replaceable. Use public incidents, hidden faults, and complete native/bundled baselines while the system is being built. After a credible alpha, use real customers to decide which wedge deserves commercial focus.

The company succeeds only when qualified operators repeatedly pay for outcomes they cannot obtain as quickly, cheaply, or safely from their complete existing workflow. The project succeeds as a career artifact only when the implementation is real, reproducible, and defensible by the founder. Acquisition becomes plausible only after operational dependence exists.
